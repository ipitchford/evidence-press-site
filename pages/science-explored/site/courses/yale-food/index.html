The Psychology, Biology and Politics of Food

Open Yale Courses · 13 catalogue entries

Open Yale Courses ·

Original course and syllabus ↗

  1. Lecture 1 - Introduction: What We Eat, Why We Eat and the Key Role of Food in Modern Life

    Frames eating as a topic involving biological preferences, psychological meanings, marketing and the wider organisation of food systems.

  2. Lecture 2 - Food Then, Food Now: Modern Food Conditions and Their Mismatch with Evolution

    Presents an evolutionary-mismatch explanation of why preferences shaped in earlier food environments may operate differently in modern ones.

  3. Lecture 3 - Biology, Nutrition and Health I: What We Eat

    Examines ways of measuring food intake, differences between individual and population measures, and errors in reporting portions and nutrition.

  4. Lecture 4 - Biology, Nutrition and Health II: What Helps Us and Hurts Us

    Reviews difficulties in researching diet–health relationships and translating that research into dietary recommendations.

  5. Lecture 5 - Biology, Nutrition and Health III: The Psychology of Taste and Addiction

    Explores taste, biological influences on eating and body-weight regulation, including methods used to investigate genetic contributions.

  6. Lecture 6 - Culture and the Remarkable Plasticity of Eating (Presentation by Ashley Gearhardt)

    Combines Ashley Gearhardt's presentation of an addiction model of eating with Kelly Brownell's discussion of cultural food practices.

  7. Lecture 7 - Hunger in the World of Plenty

    Discusses measuring hunger, consequences of starvation and the geopolitical circumstances that affect access to food.

  8. Lecture 8 - Nutrition Transition and Global Food Issues

    Examines changing diets, activity patterns and disease burdens in a global context, introducing case studies from China, India, South Africa and Finland.

  9. Lecture 12 - Public Health vs. Medical Models in Nutrition Change: Saving Lives One or a Million at a Time

    Contrasts population prevention with individual medical approaches and introduces levels of prevention, study methods and standards of proof.

  10. Lecture 13 - Eating Disorders and Obesity (Guest Lecture by B. Timothy Walsh)

    Timothy Walsh discusses eating disorders, clinical complications, diagnostic problems and explanations involving interacting vulnerabilities.

  11. Lecture 15 - Economics, Nutrition and Health: Subsidies, Food Deserts and More

    Discusses how subsidies, prices, neighbourhood conditions and poverty can shape food access and choices.

  12. Lecture 16 - Everyone but Me: The Pervasive Reach and Powerful Influence of Food Marketing on Food Choices

    Examines food advertising, character licensing, product placement and marketing within schools, with particular concern for children.

  13. Lecture 20 - Schools and Nutrition: Where Health and Politics Collide (Guest Lecture by Marlene B. Schwartz)

    Marlene Schwartz discusses school-food policy, research on policy changes and her experience as a parent advocate.
