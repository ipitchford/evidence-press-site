MIT 5.61 Physical Chemistry, Fall 2017

  1. Lecture 1: Quantum Mechanics—Historical Background, Photoelectric Effect, Compton Scattering

    Description: In this lecture, Prof. Field explains the structure of the course, historical background, and the photoelectric effect. Instructor: Prof. Robert Field

  2. Lecture 2: Wave Nature of the Electron and the Internal Structure of an Atom

    Description : This lecture covers the wave nature of the electron and the internal structure of an atom. Instructor : Prof. Robert Field

  3. Lecture 3: Two-Slit Experiment, Quantum Weirdness

    Description : This lecture covers the two-slit experiment, which demonstrates that light and matter can be defined both as waves and particles. The instructor also explains the uncertainty principle. Instructor : Prof. Robert Field

  4. Lecture 4: Classical Wave Equation and Separation of Variables

    Description : This lecture covers classical wave equations, the separation of variables, boundary conditions, and the superposition of normal modes: “the pluck.” Instructor : Prof. Robert Field

  5. Lecture 5: Quantum Mechanics: Free Particle and Particle in 1D box

    Description **:** This lecture covers free particle and particle in a 1D box, part of quantum mechanics. Instructor: Prof. Robert Field

  6. Lecture 6: 3-D Box and QM Separation of Variables

    Description: This lecture covers the concepts of the 3-D box and QM separation of variables. Instructor: Prof. Robert Field

  7. Lecture 7: Classical Mechanical Harmonic Oscillator

    Description: This lecture covers the classical mechanic treatment of the harmonic oscillator, the traditional quantum mechanic treatment, and time-dependent quantum mechanics. Instructor: Prof. Robert Field

  8. Lecture 8: Quantum Mechanical Harmonic Oscillator

    Description: This lecture covers the quantum mechanical treatment of the harmonic oscillator. Instructor: Prof. Robert Field

  9. Lecture 9: The Harmonic Oscillator: Creation and Annihilation Operators

    Description: This lecture continues the discussion of harmonic oscillators and includes information on creation and annihilation operators. Instructor: Prof. Robert Field

  10. Lecture 10: The Time-Dependent Schrödinger Equation

    Description: This lecture focuses on the time-dependent Schrödinger equation. Instructor: Prof. Robert Field

  11. Lecture 11: Wavepacket Dynamics for Harmonic Oscillator and PIB

    Description: This lecture covers wavepacket dynamics for harmonic oscillator and particle-in-a-box. Instructor: Prof. Robert Field

  12. Lecture 12: Catch Up and Review, Postulates

    Description: This lecture covers five postulates and a catch up and review before the exam. Instructor: Prof. Robert Field

  13. Lecture 13: From Hij Integrals to H Matrices I

    Description: This lecture covers the topic of Hij integrals and H matrices. Instructor: Prof. Robert Field

  14. Lecture 14: From Hij Integrals to H Matrices II

    Description: This lecture continues the topic of Hij integrals and H matrices. Instructor: Prof. Robert Field

  15. Lecture 15: Non-Degenerate Perturbation Theory I

    Description: This lecture introduces the idea of the non-degenerate perturbation theory. Instructor: Prof. Robert Field

  16. Lecture 16: Non-Degenerate Perturbation Theory II: HO using a,a†

    Description: This lecture continues exploring non-degenerate perturbation theory. Instructor: Prof. Robert Field

  17. Lecture 17: Rigid Rotor I. Orbital Angular Momentum

    Description: This lecture covers the rigid rotor and is a first glimpse at central force problems. Instructor: Prof. Robert Field

  18. Lecture 18: Rigid Rotor II. Derivation by Commutation Rules

    Description: This lecture covers the rigid rotor and derivation by commutation rules. Angular momentum is a central theme of this lecture. Instructor: Prof. Robert Field

  19. Lecture 19: Spectroscopy: Probing Molecules with Light

    Description: This lecture discusses time-dependent quantum mechanics. Instructor: Prof. Robert Field

  20. Lecture 20: Hydrogen Atom I

    Description: The lecture discusses what we can learn about molecules by looking at the hydrogen atom. Instructor: Prof. Robert Field

  21. Lecture 21: Hydrogen Atom II. Rydberg States

    Description: In this lecture, Professor Field talks about the hydrogen atom as a model for electronic structure. In particular, he discusses quantum number scaling effects. Instructor: Prof. Robert Field

  22. Lecture 22: Helium Atom

    Description: After discussing the hydrogen atom in the previous lecture, Prof. Field explores the next simplest system: the helium atom. Instructor: Prof. Robert Field

  23. Lecture 23: Many-Electron Atoms

    Description: This lecture explains how to evaluate matrix elements of many-electron operators, which is key to interpreting the electronic properties of atoms and molecules. Instructor: Prof. Robert Field

  24. Lecture 24: Molecular Orbital Theory I. Variational Principle and Matrix Mechanics

    Description: This lecture begins the discussion on molecules and molecular orbital theory. Topics include the variational method, Born-Oppenheimer Approximation, and bonding antibonding. Instructor: Prof. Robert Field

  25. Lecture 25: Molecular Orbital Theory II. H2+, A2, AB Diatomics

    Description: This lecture continues the discussion of molecular orbital theory and includes information about diatomic molecules. Instructor: Prof. Robert Field

  26. Lecture 26: Qualitative MO Theory: Hückel

    Description: This lecture explains the Hückel molecular orbital theory, which determines the energies of molecular orbitals of electrons. Instructor: Prof. Robert Field

  27. Lecture 27: Non-Degenerate Perturbation Theory III

    Description: In this lecture, Prof. Field continues the discussion of non-degenerate perturbation theory. Instructor: Prof. Robert Field

  28. Lecture 28: Modern Electronic Structure Theory: Basis Sets

    Description: Guest lecturer, Prof. Troy Van Voorhis, explores electronic structure theory. This discussion includes the topic of basis sets, which are a pre-defined set of AO basis functions for each atom in the periodic table. Instructor: Prof. Troy Van Voorh

  29. Lecture 29: Modern Electronic Structure Theory: Electronic Correlation

    Description: This lecture covers information about modern electronic structure theory. Ways of computing the energy and computational chemistry tools are discussed. Instructor: Prof. Troy Van Voorhis

  30. Lecture 30: Time-Dependent Perturbation Theory I. H is Time-Independent, Zewail Wavepacket

    Description: This is the first of two lectures on spectroscopy and dynamics. Instructor: Prof. Robert Field

  31. Lecture 31: Time-Dependent Perturbation Theory II. H is Time-Dependent: Two-Level Problem

    Description: This is the second of two lectures that focuses on spectroscopy and dynamics. Instructor: Prof. Robert Field

  32. Lecture 32: Intermolecular Interactions by Non-Degenerate Perturbation Theory

    Description: This lecture discusses wavepacket dynamics and what to expect and how to approach it. Instructor: Prof. Robert Field

  33. Lecture 33: Electronic Spectroscopy: Franck-Condon

    Description: This lecture covers intermolecular interactions, electronic spectroscopy, and the Franck-Condon principle. Instructor: Prof. Robert Field

  34. Lecture 34: Electronic Spectroscopy and Photochemistry

    Description: In this lecture, Prof. Robert field explains electronic spectroscopy and photochemistry. Instructor: Prof. Robert Field

  35. Lecture 35: δ-Functions, Eigenfunctions of X, Discrete Variable Representation

    Description: In this lecture, Prof. Field talks about the discrete variable representation. He introduces the students to delta-functions. Instructor: Prof. Robert Field

  36. Lecture 36: Time Dependence of Two-Level Systems: Density Matrix, Rotating Wave Approximation

    Description: In this final lecture, Prof. Field explains time dependence of two-level systems, with attention to density matrix and rotating wave approximation. Instructor: Prof. Robert Field
