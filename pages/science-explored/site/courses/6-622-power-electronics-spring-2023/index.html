MIT 6.622 Power Electronics, Spring 2023

  1. Lecture 1: Introduction to Power Electronics

    The primary function of power electronic circuits is the processing and control of electrical energy. This class discusses the history, evolution, needs, and goals of power electronics.

  2. Lecture 2: Analysis Methods and Rectifiers

    In this lecture, we first review linear vs. switching regulators. We then introduce definitions and methods for analyzing switching circuits, including the method of assumed states and periodic steady state.

  3. Lecture 3: Load Regulation

    In this lecture, we discuss rectifiers and their DC-side characteristics. As part of this we consider how load regulation in rectifiers is introduced by the effect of AC-side commutating reactance.

  4. Lecture 4: Power Factor

    This lecture considers power factors and measures of distortion in AC systems.

  5. Lecture 5: Intro to DC/DC, Part 1

    This class introduces DC-DC power conversion, including methods for analyzing DC-DC converters in periodic steady state. Buck and boost converters are introduced as examples.

  6. Lecture 6: DC/DC, Part 2

  7. Lecture 7: DC/DC, Part 3

    In this lecture, we show how the device and passive component ratings of a converter may be analyzed, describe techniques for modeling converter ripple, and relate this to component selection.

  8. Lecture 8: DC/DC, Part 4

    This lecture expands upon the topic of converter waveform ripple and ripple ratio, and explains the discontinuous conduction mode (DCM) of converters.

  9. Lecture 9: Magnetics, Part 1

    This lecture reviews basic concepts of magnetics and their use in analyzing inductors. The concept of a magnetic circuit model is introduced, including magneto-motive force (MMF) and reluctance.

  10. Lecture 10: Magnetics, Part 2

    This lecture reviews the concept of a magnetic circuit model, and leverages magnetic circuits to describe the characteristics of a practical transformer, including leakage and magnetizing inductances.

  11. Lecture 11: Magnetics, Part 3

    This lecture reviews the modeling of practical 2-winding transformers with circuit models and inductance matrices, and expands these concepts to many-winding transformers, including the introduction of the extended cantilever model.

  12. Lecture 12: Magnetics, Part 4

    We care about loss in magnetics both for converter efficiency and because the loss often sets the required size of a magnetic component. Here we consider both core loss and winding loss effects that are important in practice.

  13. Lecture 13: Isolated DC/DC Converters, Part 1

    This lecture explores the use of transformers to realize isolated DC-DC converters. The flyback converter and forward converter are introduced as examples.

  14. Lecture 14: Isolated DC/DC Converters, Part 2

    Building on previous discussions of isolated converters, we explore multiple outputs, transformer leakage inductances, and double-ended converters.

  15. Lecture 15: Switching Losses and Snubbers

  16. Lecture 16: Thermal Modeling and Heat Sinking

    This lecture considers thermal modeling and heat sinking of power electronics.

  18. Lecture 18: Inverters, Part 2

    Harmonic cancellation and harmonic elimination techniques are introduced for controlling the output waveform content of single-phase inverters.

  19. Lecture 19: Inverters, Part 3

    This lecture introduces multilevel inverters that are suitable for realizing harmonic elimination and cancellation, and introduces pulse width modulation techniques for inverters.

  20. Lecture 20: Switched-Mode Rectifiers

    This lecture introduces how one can realize switched-mode rectifiers to draw and control energy from the grid with high power factor.

  21. Lecture 21: Three-Phase Systems, Part 1

    This lecture discusses the origins of polyphase and why we use 3-phase systems.

  22. Lecture 22: Three-Phase Systems, Part 2

    This lecture introduces the three-phase diode bridge rectifier and higher-order rectifiers. We also describe how we can use transformers to generate phase-shifted voltage sets for high-order rectifiers.

  23. Lecture 23: Three-Phase Inverters

    In this lecture, we consider the implementation of three-phase inverters and describe how pulse-width modulation may be realized for three-phase systems.

  24. Lecture 24: Control, Part 1

    This lecture introduces basic concepts of dynamic control and modeling of DC-DC converters. The local average operator is introduced to describe the averaged behavior of a DC-DC converter through direct circuit averaging.

  25. Lecture 25: Control, Part 2

    This lecture introduces the concept of state space averaging for modeling switching converters. The notion of linearization of nonlinear averaged models is introduced to realize control-to-output transfer functions.

  26. Lecture 26: Control, Part 3

    This lecture provides an example of control modeling and feedback design of a DC-DC boost converter, highlighting the aspects that must be considered in a control design.

  27. Lecture 27: Current-Mode Control

    In this lecture, we introduce current-mode control (CMC), in which we control a converter based on both inductor current and output (capacitor) voltage. Methods for generating linearized, averaged models of peak current-mode controlled converters are described

  28. Lecture 28: EMI Filters, Part 1

  29. Lecture 29: EMI Filters, Part 2

    This lecture looks at important elements of EMI filter design, including component and layout parasitics, the need for and optimization of filter damping, and the effect of the converter on filter damping.

  30. Lecture 30: EMI Filters, Part 3: CM + DM

    This lecture expands our consideration of EMI filtering to introduce the concepts of common-mode (CM) and differential-mode (DM) signals and how filters are designed to handle CM and DM noise.

  31. Lecture 31: Switched-Capacitor Convertors, Part 1

  32. Lecture 32: Switched-Capacitor Convertors, Part 2

    This lecture expands upon the description of switched capacitor converters, detailing charging/discharging loss of capacitors and describing different kinds of switched capacitors and related circuits.

  33. Lecture 33: Soft Switching, Part 1

    Soft-switching techniques try to mitigate switching loss and noise in power electronics. General methods include “zero voltage” switching (ZVS) and “zero current” switching (ZCS).

  34. Lecture 34: Soft Switching, Part 2

  35. Lecture 35: Resonant Power Conversion, Part 1

    Resonant converters and inverters operate using resonant circuits with high-frequency sinusoidal waveforms. To introduce this topic, this lecture reviews resonant circuits, using the series LRC circuit as an example.

  36. Lecture 36: Resonant Power Conversion, Part 2

    This lecture introduces the basics of resonant power converters. The fundamental harmonic approximation (FHA) method for analyzing resonant converters is introduced, and the characteristics of various resonant tanks, inverters, and rectifiers are described.

  37. Lecture 37: Resonant Converters: Matching Networks

    This lecture introduces resonant techniques for scaling voltage and current through the use of matching networks. The characteristics of matching networks are described.

  38. Lecture 38: Gate Drive, Level Shift, Layout

    This lecture describes some practical aspects of power electronics design, including circuit layout.
