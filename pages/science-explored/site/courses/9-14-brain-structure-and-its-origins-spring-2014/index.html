Brain Structure and Its Origins

MIT OpenCourseWare · Spring 2014

Original course and syllabus ↗

MIT 9.14 Brain Structure and Its Origins, Spring 2014

  1. 1. Introduction: brain orientation, primitive cellular activities

    Description: This lecture introduces key anatomical terms and primitive cellular mechanisms that are retained in the evolution of neurons. Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider

  2. 2. Introduction: methods and primitive cellular activities

    Description: This lecture continues to introduce the methods for mapping pathways and interconnections that enable the integrative activity of the CNS. Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider

  3. 3. Steps to the CNS of chordates, part 1

    Description: This lecture discusses the evolution of multicellular organisms and the initial steps to the CNS of advanced chordates. Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider

  4. 4. Steps to the CNS of chordates, part 2

    Description: This lecture completes the discussion about the evolution of multicellular organisms and the initial steps to the CNS of advanced chordates. Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider

  5. 5. Specializations in the evolving CNS, part 1

    Description: This lecture begins the discussion of specializations in the evolving CNS, including sketching the brain and basic pathways of ancestral mammals. Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider

  6. 6. Specializations in the evolving CNS, part 2

    Description: This lecture concludes the discussion of specializations in the evolving CNS, including sketching the brain and basic pathways of ancestral mammals. Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider

  7. 7. Spinal cord development and anatomy, part 1

    Description: This lecture introduces the development and differentiation of the spinal cord following gastrulation. Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider

  8. 8. Spinal cord development and anatomy, part 2

    Description: This lecture completes the development and differentiation of the spinal cord, including CNS structures and the autonomic and enteric nervous systems. Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider

  9. 9. Autonomic nervous system; differentiation of the brain vesicles and intro to the hindbrain and segmentation

    Description: This lecture covers the autonomic nervous system as well as the differentiation of the brain vesicles and introduces the hindbrain and segmentation. Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider

  10. 10. Differentiation of the brain vesicles, part 1: Hindbrain and cranial nerves, specializations

    Description: This lecture discusses the differentiation of the brain vesicles, focusing on the hindbrain and cranial nerves. Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider

  11. 11. Differentiation of the brain vesicles, part 2: Developmental distortions; evolution of midbrain and forebrain; midbrain organization

    Description: This lecture discusses the differentiation of the brain vesicles, including developmental distortions, the midbrain and the forebrain. Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider

  12. 12. Differentiation of the brain vesicles, part 3: Vertebrate forebrain evolution and organization

    Description: This lecture concludes the discussion of the differentiation of the brain vesicles, focusing on vertebrate forebrain evolution and organization. Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider

  13. 14. CNS differentiation and axon growth, part 2

    Description: This lecture completes the discussion about CNS differentiation and axon growth, including growth factors, chemoaffinity and additional roles for neurotrophins. Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider

  14. 15. Motor systems and brain states, part 1

    Description: This lecture is the first of four on motor systems and brain state, and finishes the discussion of brachium bridges and introduces motor system structure. Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider

  15. 16. Motor systems and brain states, part 2

    Description: This lecture is the second of four on motor systems and brain states, and focuses on descending pathways and evolution. Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider

  16. 17. Motor systems and brain states, part 3

    Description: This lecture is the third of four on motor systems and brain state, and covers temporal patterns and brain state changes. Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider

  17. 18. Motor systems and brain states, part 4

    Description: This lecture is the fourth of four on motor systems and brain states, and focuses on brain state changes. Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider

  18. 21. Visual systems, part 1

    Description: This lecture is the first of three on visual systems, including the origins of vision. Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider

  19. 22. Visual systems, part 2

    Description: This lecture is the second of three on visual systems, focusing on retinal projections, light detection and species differences in structures. Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider

  20. 23. Visual systems, part 3

    Description: This lecture is the third of three on visual systems with a discussion on topographic organization of the midbrain and hindbrain. Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider

  21. 24. Auditory systems, part 1

    Description: This lecture is the first of two on auditory systems, but also includes a summary discussion of the visual systems. Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider

  22. 25. Auditory systems, part 2

    Description: This lecture is the second of two on auditory systems and focuses on two ascending pathways and specializations. Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider

  23. 26. Forebrain evolution

    Description: This lecture focuses on forebrain evolution, though it begins with a summary of auditory specializations. Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider

  24. 27. Hypothalamus and limbic system, part 1

    Description: This lecture is the first of six on the hypothalamus and limbic system and covers the evolution of the forebrain and endbrain. Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider

  25. 28. Hypothalamus and limbic system, part 2

    Description: Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider

  26. 29. Hypothalamus and limbic system, part 3

    Description: This lecture is the third of six on the hypothalamus and limbic system and covers the core pathways and anatomy. Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider

  27. 30. Hypothalamus and limbic system, part 4

    Description: This lecture is the fourth of six on the hypothalamus and limbic system and focuses on the influence of hormones on brain development and plasticity. Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider

  28. 31. Hypothalamus and limbic system, part 5

    Description: This lecture is the fifth of six on the hypothalamus and limbic system and focuses on the hippocampus. Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider

  29. 32. Hypothalamus and limbic system, part 6

    Description: This lecture is the sixth of six on the hypothalamus and limbic system and focuses on the the limbic forebrain and amygdala. Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider

  30. 33. Corpus striatum, part 1

    Description: This lecture is the first of two on the corpus striatum that includes a summary of the limbic system and introduces the corpus striatum. Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider

  31. 34. Corpus striatum, part 2

    Description: This lecture is the second of two on the corpus striatum, including discussion of the anatomy, connections and associated diseases. Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider

  32. 35. Neocortex, part 1

    Description: This lecture is the first of three on the neocortex, including a summary of diseases of the corpus striatum and introduction of the neocortex evolution and anatomy. Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider

  33. 36. Neocortex, part 2

    Description: This lecture is the second of three on the neocortex and covers the functions, cell types, and connections of the neocortex. Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider

  34. 37. Neocortex, part 3

    Description: This lecture is the third of three on the neocortex and covers the fiber architecture, thalamocortical organization and connections of neocortex. Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider

  35. 38. Plasticity in brain connections

    Description: This lecture is the last in the course and summarizes brain development and plasticity. Instructor: Gerard E. Schneider
