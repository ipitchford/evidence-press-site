Descriptive Statistics with R Software

NPTEL — National Programme on Technology Enhanced Learning · 12 catalogue entries

NPTEL — National Programme on Technology Enhanced Learning ·

Original course and syllabus ↗

  1. Lecture 06 : Objectives, Steps and Basic Definitions

    Introduces the purpose of descriptive statistics, the distinction between a sample and a population, and numerical and graphical ways to summarise variation. The instructor explains that the course focuses on implementing these tools in R.

  2. Lecture 07 : Variables and Types of Data

    Explains types of variables and data using examples such as clothing sizes and prices. The inspected passages also distinguish sources of data, including surveys and primary and secondary collection.

  3. Lecture 08 : Absolute Frequency, Relative Frequency and Frequency Distribution

    Builds absolute and relative frequency tables, moving from category counts to proportions. The checked R material shows how table and length operations implement the denominator used in the summary.

  4. Lecture 09 : Frequency Distribution and Cumulative Distribution Function

    Groups observations into intervals and constructs frequency and cumulative-frequency summaries. The inspected race-time example uses R operations such as cut and table to show how grouping decisions become calculations.

  5. Lecture 10 : Bar Diagrams

    Creates bar diagrams from frequency tables and relative frequencies. The inspected examples show how category counts, denominators, labels and graphical parameters affect the resulting display in R.

  6. Lecture 12 : 3D Pie Diagram and Histogram

    Demonstrates three-dimensional pie diagrams and then introduces histograms for grouped numerical data. The inspected histogram passage distinguishes the roles of bar height and width, followed by R labelling options.

  7. Lecture 14 : Arithmetic Mean

    Develops the arithmetic mean for raw and grouped observations, including weighted calculations. The inspected R examples address missing entries and show that the values included in the calculation determine the result.

  8. Lecture 15 : Median

    Explains the median through ordered observations and partitioning a distribution. The inspected material compares grouped calculations with R output and demonstrates the treatment of missing entries.

  9. Lecture 16 : Quantiles

    Introduces quantiles, quartiles and percentiles, including the difference between a value and its position in a distribution. The checked R material exposes options for probabilities, calculation type and missing observations.

  10. Lecture 18 : Range, Interquartile Range and Quartile Deviation

    Shows why distributions with a similar centre can have different spreads. The inspected examples calculate the range and interquartile range in R and discuss how these summaries use different parts of the observations.

  11. Lecture 25 : Univariate and Bivariate Scatter Plots

    Introduces scatterplots for paired numerical observations, including study-hours and marks examples. The inspected R sequence discusses labels, plot types and display choices when showing a relationship.

  12. Lecture 28 : Correlation Coefficient

    Introduces covariance and the correlation coefficient as descriptions of a linear relationship between two numerical variables. The inspected passages use cross-products and discuss the symmetry of the association.
