Introduction to Psychology

Open Yale Courses · 17 catalogue entries

Open Yale Courses · 2007

Original course and syllabus ↗

  1. Lecture 2 - Foundations: This Is Your Brain

    Introduces dualism and materialism as contrasting accounts of mind and body, followed by basic brain neurophysiology.

  2. Lecture 3 - Foundations: Freud

    Paul Bloom introduces Freud’s psychoanalytic ideas, their limitations and the historical role of unconscious mental processes in psychology.

  3. Lecture 4 - Foundations: Skinner

    Introduces Skinner and behaviourism, compares forms of learning and discusses why behaviourism alone is insufficient for the course’s account of mental life.

  4. Lecture 5 - What Is It Like to Be a Baby: The Development of Thought

    Introduces Piaget’s approach and the study of infant cognition, then asks how different kinds of development relate.

  5. Lecture 6 - How Do We Communicate?: Language in the Brain, Mouth and the Hands

    Introduces language through sounds, word structure, sentence structure and recursion, alongside arguments about acquisition and specialisation.

  6. Lecture 7 - Conscious of the Present; Conscious of the Past: Language (cont.); Vision and Memory

    Paul Bloom moves from animal communication and language–thought questions into perception, attention and different kinds of memory.

  7. Lecture 8 - Conscious of the Present; Conscious of the Past: Vision and Memory (cont.)

    Reviews memory types, limitations, strategies and disorders, then discusses suggestibility and the social interpretation of memory research.

  8. Lecture 10 - Evolution, Emotion, and Reason: Evolution and Rationality

    Introduces an evolutionary perspective on psychology and discusses how evolutionary arguments are used to constrain explanations of behaviour and choice.

  9. Lecture 11 - Evolution, Emotion, and Reason: Emotions, Part I

    Presents an evolutionary approach to emotion through examples of smiling, fear, family relationships and infants' responses to caregivers.

  10. Lecture 12 - Evolution, Emotion, and Reason: Emotions, Part II

    Continues an evolutionary interpretation of social emotions, discussing parental love, gratitude, responses to cheating and cultural differences in revenge.

  11. Lecture 13 - Why Are People Different?: Differences

    Surveys personality and intelligence measurement and the course’s discussion of genetic and environmental explanations for differences between people.

  12. Lecture 14 - What Motivates Us: Sex

    Presents a 2007 course discussion of evolutionary explanations and psychological studies concerning attraction, sex, gender differences and sexual orientation.

  13. Lecture 16 - A Person in the World of People: Self and Other, Part I

    Introduces social psychology through connectedness and biases in thinking about oneself and other people.

  14. Lecture 17 - A Person in the World of People: Self and Other, Part II; Some Mysteries: Sleep, Dreams, and Laughter

    Combines a discussion of social impressions and stereotypes with an introduction to sleep, dreams and theories about laughter.

  15. Lecture 18 - What Happens When Things Go Wrong: Mental Illness, Part I (Guest Lecture by Professor Susan Nolen-Hoeksema)

    Susan Nolen-Hoeksema introduces clinical identification and treatment with particular attention to mood disorders, including depression and bipolar disorder.

  16. Lecture 19 - What Happens When Things Go Wrong: Mental Illness, Part II

    Discusses definitions of mental illness and several diagnostic groupings before introducing therapy.

  17. Lecture 20 - The Good Life: Happiness

    Asks how treatment efficacy can be assessed, then turns to happiness, cultural variation and people's predictions about future satisfaction.
