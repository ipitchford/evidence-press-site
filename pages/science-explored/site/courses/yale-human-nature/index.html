Philosophy and the Science of Human Nature

Open Yale Courses · 12 catalogue entries

Open Yale Courses · 2011

Original course and syllabus ↗

  1. Lecture 1 - Introduction

    Tamar Gendler introduces a course connecting philosophy, psychology, behavioural economics and literature through flourishing, morality and political thought.

  2. Lecture 3 - Parts of the Soul I

    Compares several historical and modern ways of dividing motives or mental processes, including accounts associated with Plato, Hume, Freud and Haidt.

  3. Lecture 4 - Parts of the Soul II

    Introduces sampling bias, dual-process accounts, the Wason task and framing examples before explaining Gendler’s distinction between alief and belief.

  4. Lecture 9 - Virtue and Habit I

    Connects Aristotle’s account of acquiring virtue with habituation and comparisons to conditioning and parenting practices.

  5. Lecture 10 - Virtue and Habit II

    Continues Aristotle’s account of virtue, discusses pleasure and flow, and introduces a situationist challenge to stable character.

  6. Lecture 11 - Weakness of the Will and Procrastination

    Discusses weakness of will, delay of gratification and discounting, then presents several proposed ways of structuring self-regulation.

  7. Lecture 12 - Utilitarianism and its Critiques

    Introduces moral theories and examines Mill’s utilitarianism through a dilemma about aggregate happiness and individual suffering.

  8. Lecture 13 - Deontology

    Contrasts a criticism of utilitarianism with an introduction to Kant’s account of moral duty and the agent’s will.

  9. Lecture 14 - The Trolley Problem

    Continues the categorical imperative and introduces the trolley problem through Foot’s and Thomson’s proposed responses.

  10. Lecture 15 - Empirically-informed Responses

    Compares three non-classic responses to the trolley problem that use different methods to examine apparently conflicting judgements.

  11. Lecture 16 - Philosophical Puzzles

    Discusses heuristics in risk regulation and then introduces puzzles about shielding, ducking and moral luck.

  12. Lecture 20 - The Prisoner's Dilemma

    Examines the prisoners’ dilemma and the commons as cases where individually attractive choices can produce a collectively worse result.
