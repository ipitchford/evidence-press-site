MIT 9.13 The Human Brain, Spring 2019

  1. Lecture 1: Introduction

    Summary: Prof. Kanwisher tells a true story to introduce the course, then covers the why, how, and what of studying the human brain, and gives a course overview. Speaker: Nancy Kanwisher

  2. Lecture 2: Neuroanatomy

    Summary: Basic brief neuroanatomy review in preparation for dissection, including an introduction to the cortex, primary regions, and topographic maps. Speaker: Nancy Kanwisher

  3. Lecture 4: Cognitive Neuroscience Methods I

    Summary: Introduction to methods in cognitive neuroscience including computation, behavior, fMRI, ERPs & MEG, neuropsychology patients, TMS, and intracranial recordings in humans and nonhuman primates. Speaker: Nancy Kanwisher

  4. Lecture 5: Cognitive Neuroscience Methods II

    Summary: Continuation of discussion of methods in cognitive neuroscience including computation, behavior, fMRI, ERPs & MEG, neuropsychology patients, TMS, and intracranial recordings in humans and nonhuman primates. Speaker: Nancy Kanwisher

  5. Lecture 6: Experimental Design

    Summary: This session reviews the last two lectures, face recognition, and explores types of experimental methods. Speaker: Nancy Kanwisher

  6. Lecture 7: Category Selectivity, Controversies, and MVPA

    Summary: Covers controversies and alternative views of the ventral visual pathway, multiple voxel pattern analysis, and the two visual pathways. Speaker: Nancy Kanwisher

  7. Lecture 8: Navigation I

    Summary: The functional organization of scene perception and navigation and the various brain structures that implement them. Speaker: Nancy Kanwisher

  8. Lecture 9: Navigation II

    Summary: Scene perception and navigation continued. Speaker: Nancy Kanwisher

  9. Lecture 10: Development, Nature & Nurture I

    Summary: This lecture examines how we think the cortex organizes in the brain over infancy and childhood, and the function of genes vs experience. Speaker: Nancy Kanwisher

  10. Lecture 11: Development, Nature & Nurture II (2018)

    Summary: Continues the discussion of genes vs experience on cortical organization, and whether the cortex can change in adulthood. Speaker: Nancy Kanwisher

  11. Lecture 16: Music

    Summary: This session explores the functional organization of music in human beings. Speaker: Nancy Kanwisher

  12. Lecture 18: Language I

    Summary: Covers the basic organization of language in the brain and the long-standing question of the relationship between thought and language. Speaker: Nancy Kanwisher

  13. Lecture 21: Brain Networks

    Summary: Looks at the major white matter tracts in the human brain, predicting function and correlations between regions. Speaker: Nancy Kanwisher

  14. Lecture 13: Number

    Summary: Explores the nature of the human representation of number and how it is implemented in the brain. Speaker: Nancy Kanwisher

  15. Lecture 15: Hearing and Speech

    Summary: Humans use hearing in species-specific ways, for speech and music. Ongoing research is working out the functional organization of these and other human auditory skills. Speaker: Nancy Kanwisher

  16. Lecture 20: Mentalizing and Theory of Mind

    Summary: The ability of humans to think about what other people are thinking is implemented in brain regions highly specialized for this function alone. Speaker: Nancy Kanwisher

  17. Lecture 24: Attention and Awareness

    Summary: Looks at the differences in the mind and brain between perceptual information we are aware of versus information we are not. Speaker: Nancy Kanwisher
