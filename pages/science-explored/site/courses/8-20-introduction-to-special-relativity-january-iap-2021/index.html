MIT 8.20 Introduction to Special Relativity, January IAP 2021

  1. Lecture 1.1: Course Organization

    Description: Discussion of the course outline and setup, grading scheme, and first introduction to the concept of relativity. (19:07) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  2. Lecture 1.2: Prof. Klute’s Research

    Description: A very brief introduction to Prof. Klute’s research and work at the Large Hadron Collider. (13:22) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  3. Lecture 1.3: History of Special Relativity

    Description: The historic backdrop for special relativity. (10:46) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  4. Lecture 1.4: Space, Time, and Spacetime

    Description: Professor David Kaiser teaches in the physics department at MIT and is also a historian of science. In this guest lecture, he describes how the most accomplished physicists of the mid-to-late 19 th century were thinking about motion of bodies thro

  5. Lecture 1.5: Categories of Physics

    Description: Special relativity is a complete description of physical phenomena at high speed. In this short video we discuss how it fits in with other categories of physics. (03:31) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  6. Lecture 2.1: Events

    Description: We define the principle of relativity in this section and make a difference between events and frames used to describe them. (03:32) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  7. Lecture 2.2: Galilean Transformation

    Description: In Newton’s mechanics, t and a are invariant and no inertial frame is above others. (06:14) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  8. Lecture 3.1: Light

    Description: We start the discussion of light. What is it and how does it propagate? (07:27) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  9. Lecture 3.2: Waves

    Description: Discussion of properties of waves. (08:51) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  10. Lecture 3.3: Michelson-Morley Experiment

    Description: We are building a case against the ether hypothesis starting with the Michelson-Morley experiment. (05:38) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  11. Lecture 3.4: Stellar Aberration

    Description: We continue to make a case against ether by studying stellar aberration. (03:54) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  12. Lecture 4.1: Time Dilation

    Description: Time is suspect. We analyze the consequences of Einstein’s postulate and find time dilation. (13:38) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  13. Lecture 4.2: Muons

    Description: A brief introduction to muons and a discussion of time dilation extending the lifetime of particles. (04:47) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  14. Lecture 4.3: Length Contraction

    Description: We derive length contraction. (03:29) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  15. Lecture 4.4: Invariance

    Description: We summarize briefly and then discuss invariant observables. (05:49) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  16. Lecture 4.5: Lorentz Transformation

    Description: We derive the Lorentz transformation. (06:54) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  17. Lecture 5.1: Voyager Program

    Description: In this short section, we take a journey to Alpha Centauri. (03:36) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  18. Lecture 5.2: Velocity Addition

    Description: We investigate how velocities in two reference frames compare. (06:18) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  19. Lecture 5.3: Spacetime Diagrams

    Description: Introduction to spacetime diagrams. (08:00) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  20. Lecture 5.4: Regions in Spacetime Diagrams

    Description: We introduce time-like, space-like, and light-like regions in spacetime diagrams. (04:32) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  21. Lecture 5.5: Causality

    Description: We discuss cause and effect. (05:37) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  22. Lecture 6.1: Pole-in-the-Barn Paradox

    Description: We discuss the pole-in-the-barn paradox. (07:17) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  23. Lecture 6.2: Twin Paradox

    Description: We discuss the twin paradox. (06:07) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  24. Lecture 6.3: Spacecraft-on-a-Rope Paradox

    Description: We discuss how objects contract and the spacecraft-on-a-rope paradox. (04:35) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  25. Lecture 7.1: Introduction to the Doppler Effect

    Description: Introduction to the classical Doppler effect. (06:13) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  26. Lecture 7.2: Relativistic Doppler Effect

    Description: We now turn to the relativistic Doppler effect. (03:19) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  27. Lecture 7.3: Redshift

    Description: We discuss the Lorentz transformation of a monochromatic plane wave and redshift. (06:03) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  28. Lecture 7.4: Galaxy Travel

    Description: What do we see when we travel the galaxy? (05:54) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  29. Lecture 8.1: Algebra of Lorentz Transformations

    Description: We talk about the algebra of Lorentz transformations. (05:07) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  30. Lecture 8.2: Introduction to 4-Vector Notation

    Description: Introduction of 4-vectors. (05:45) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  31. Lecture 8.3: Proper Velocity

    Description: Definition of proper velocity. (04:02) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  32. Lecture 9.1: Momentum Conservation

    Description: Momentum using proper velocity. (04:06) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  33. Lecture 9.2: Energy Conservation

    Description: Further investigation of the energy-momentum 4-vector. (03:43) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  34. Lecture 9.4: Forces and Kinetic Energy

    Description: Forces and kinetic energy. (06:46) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  35. Lecture 9.3: Collisions

    Description: Discussion of elastic and inelastic collisions. Short introduction of the units of energy. (07:09) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  36. Lecture 10.1: Tests of Special Relativity

    Description: Einstein’s postulates are not axioms and need to be tested. We review some of the experimental evidence. (06:33) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  37. Lecture 10.2: The Large Electron-Positron Collider

    Description: We talk about the large electron-positron collider (LEP) and the velocity of electrons and positrons. (04:21) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  38. Lecture 10.3: Deuteron Production

    Description: We discuss deuteron production. (03:17) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  39. Lecture 10.4: Absorption and Emission of Photons

    Description: We learn how photons are absorbed and emitted by an atom. (05:15) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  40. Lecture 10.5: Decay of a Pion

    Description: We study particle decays using pions as an example. (03:01) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  41. Lecture 10.6: Creation of Particles

    Description: How collisions of particles create new particles. (05:58) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  42. Lecture 10.7: Compton Effect

    Description: We investigate scattering of a photon on an electron at rest. (04:51) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  43. Lecture 10.8: Global Positioning System

    Description: Global positioning systems as an application of special relativity. (04:55) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  44. Lecture 11.1: Charge and Current

    Description: We discuss the electromagnetic effect in the context of special relativity. (06:36) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  45. Lecture 11.2: Electric and Magnetic Fields

    Description: A variety of laws explain the properties of electric and magnetic fields. (04:30) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  46. Lecture 12.1: Equivalence Principle

    Description: We discuss the equivalence of gravitational and inertial mass as discovered by Einstein. (01:58) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  47. Lecture 12.2: Time Dilation Effect on Earth

    Description: Gravitational time dilation and the effect it has on earth. (03:10) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  48. Lecture 12.3: Bending of Light

    Description: Bending of light as a consequence of general relativity and how we use a total solar eclipse to test it. (04:40) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  49. Lecture 12.4: Redshift Tests

    Description: The consequence of time dilation is a change in light frequency. (02:41) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  50. Lecture 12.5: General Relativity

    Description: How general relativity can patch together different reference points that can each be described in special relativity. (07:42) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  51. Lecture 12.6: Experimental Evidence

    Description: Experimental evidence of general relativity. (09:35) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute

  52. Lecture 13.1: Course Review

    Description: We give an overview of the material covered in 8.20 interleaved with activities. (18:13) Instructor: Prof. Markus Klute
