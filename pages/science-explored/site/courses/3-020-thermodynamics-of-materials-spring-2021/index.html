MIT 3.020 Thermodynamics of Materials, Spring 2021

  1. Lecture 1: Introduction to Thermodynamics

    This first class session introduces entropy and spontaneous processes, molecular interactions, and enthalpy, as well as the scope and use of thermodynamics.

  2. Lecture 2: Scope and Use of Thermodynamics

    This lecture covers systems, phases, states, and state functions, equations of state, compressibility and thermal expansion, and extensive vs intensive properties.

  3. Lecture 3: Process Variables and the First Law

    This lecture covers the conceptual introduction to work and heat, classifying adiabatic, isobaric, and isochronic processes, heat capacity, and the First Law.

  4. Lecture 4: Heat Engines and Energy Conversion Efficiency

    This lecture covers heat engines, the Carnot cycle with ideal gas, Carnot efficiency, and Clausius’ theorem.

  5. Lecture 5: Second Law and Entropy Maximization

    This lecture covers the second law and entropy maximization, the combined statement of the first and second laws, and equilibrium in an isolated system.

  6. Lecture 6: Thermodynamic Potentials

    This lecture covers thermodynamic potentials, chemical potential, and general strategy.

  7. Lecture 7: Ideal Gas Processes

    This lecture covers adiabatic and isothermal processes and free expansion.

  8. Lecture 9: Case Studies - Specific Heats and Phase Transformations

    This lecture looks at cases involving heat capacity and phase transformations.

  9. Supplemental Video: The Three Dees of Thermodynamics

    This lightboard video explains the different letter dees used in thermodynamics equation notation and what they represent.

  10. Lecture 12: Case Studies - Saturation Vapor Pressure

    This lecture looks at cases involving integrating the C-C equation for vaporization of a condensed phase, dew point and relative humidity, and the cloud chamber problem.

  11. Lecture 13: Introduction to Ideal (Gas) Mixtures

    This lecture covers reacting gas systems, Dalton’s rule, Gibbs free energy of an ideal gas mixture, and balancing chemical reactions.

  12. Supplemental Video: The Lever Rule

    This video explains and diagrams the lever rule.

  13. Lecture 16: Partial Molar Properties

    This lecture covers the PMP definition, Gibbs-Duhem, and calculating PMPs from solution models, including graphical solutions.

  14. Lecture 20: Introduction to Binary Phase Diagrams

    This lecture covers chemical potential in multicomponent systems, equilibrium conditions in multi-component, multi-phase systems, and the Gibbs phase rule for multicomponent, heterogeneous systems.

  15. Lecture 21: Phase Coexistence and Separation

    This lecture covers the condition for 2-phase coexistence, common tangent construction, and the spinodal phase diagram.

  16. Lecture 22: Free Energy-Composition Diagrams, General Case

    This lecture covers reference states, and drawing free energy-composition diagrams.

  17. Lecture 23: Building Binary Phase Diagrams, Part I

    This lecture covers the taut rope construction, lens diagrams, and eutectic systems.

  18. Supplemental Video: Gallium-Indium Eutectic Demo

    This video demonstrates the melting temperatures of different compositions of Gallium-Indium.

  19. Lecture 24: Building Binary Phase Diagrams, Part II

    This lecture covers peritectic systems, intermediate phases, exploring Thermo-Calc, and line compounds.

  20. Lecture 25: Building Binary Phase Diagrams, Part III

    This lecture looks at a case study modeling the Cr-Fe system detailing how to reverse-engineer a phase diagram using Thermo-Calc software.

  21. Lecture 26: CALPHAD - Case Studies and Guest Lecture

    Guest lecture by Prof. G.B. Olson that covers the CALPHAD methodology.

  22. Lecture 28: Boltzmann Hypothesis

    This lecture covers the Boltzmann hypothesis, configurational entropy, and setting up maximum entropy for an isolated system.

  23. Lecture 29: Boltzmann Distribution

    This lecture covers the Boltzmann distribution as equilibrium condition, microcanonical and canonical ensembles, and partition functions.

  24. Lecture 31: Reacting Multi-phase Systems

    This lecture covers solid-vapor equilibrium, oxidation, and Richardson-Ellingham diagrams.

  25. Lecture 32: Case Study - Reacting, Multi-component, Multi-phase Systems

    This lecture looks at cases involving a reacting system with condensed and gaseous phases.
