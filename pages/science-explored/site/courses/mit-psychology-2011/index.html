MIT 9.00SC Introduction to Psychology, Fall 2011

  1. Lecture 3: Brain I

    Introduces neurons and the organisation of the brain through neurological cases, functional specialisation and the relationship between brain activity and mental life.

  2. Lecture 4: Brain II

    Compares lesion, stimulation and recording approaches to studying the human brain, with attention to what each kind of evidence can and cannot show.

  3. Lecture 7: Attention

    Introduces visual attention, distraction and classic experimental demonstrations including visual search, object tracking and attentional limits.

  4. Lecture 9: Learning

    Surveys classical and operant conditioning, their limits, learned helplessness and delayed gratification in MIT’s 2011 introductory psychology course.

  5. Lecture 10: Memory I

    Introduces memory categories, retention and interference through the first memory session in MIT’s introductory psychology course.

  6. Lecture 11: Memory II: Amnesia and Memory Systems

    Uses amnesia and the case of H. M. to introduce distinctions among memory systems and the role of brain evidence in studying them.

  7. Lecture 12: Language

    Connects speech perception and meaning with the brain basis of language, language difficulties and acquisition, including infant and bilingual examples.

  8. Lecture 13: Thinking

    Uses problems and puzzles to examine functional fixedness, heuristics, anchoring and framing, with a brief discussion of frontal-lobe contributions.

  9. Lecture 14: Intelligence

    Examines intelligence tests and models, including test validity, biological and environmental interpretations, and debates about educational interventions.

  10. Lecture 15: Emotion & Motivation

    Introduces ways of defining emotion, including subjective feeling, bodily response, expression, valence and arousal, before considering models and brain mechanisms.

  11. Lecture 16: Personality

    Introduces ways of measuring personality and discusses trait dimensions, temperament, biological contributions and learning in MIT’s 2011 course.

  12. Lecture 17: Child Development

    Introduces infancy and cognitive development through historical stage theories, learning and questions about other minds.

  13. Lecture 18: Adult Development

    Extends developmental psychology into adolescence, adulthood and ageing, including cognitive, emotional and physical change.

  14. Lecture 19: Stress

    Distinguishes acute and chronic stressors and discusses psychological anticipation using an experimental example of waiting for an unpleasant stimulus.

  15. Lecture 20: Psychopathology I

    Introduces the history of psychiatric explanations and treatment, the purposes and consequences of diagnostic labels, and the difficulties of classifying distress.

  16. Lecture 21: Psychopathology II

    Discusses psychological and medication approaches to treatment, the testing of psychotherapy, and the lecture's examples of depression and ADHD.
