Digital Signal Processing

MIT OpenCourseWare · 22 catalogue entries

MIT OpenCourseWare · Spring 2011

Original course and syllabus ↗

MIT RES.6-008 Digital Signal Processing, 1975

  1. Lecture 1: Introduction

    Topics covered: Introduction, applications, Enhancement, processing. Instructor: Prof. Alan V. Oppenheim

  2. Demonstration 1: Sampling, Aliasing, and Frequency Response, Part 1

    Topics covered: Sampling and aliasing with a sinusoidal signal, sinusoidal response of a digital filter, dependence of frequency response on sampling period, periodic nature of the frequency response of a digital filter. Instructor: Prof. Alan V. Oppenheim

  3. Demonstration 2: Sampling, Aliasing, and Frequency Response, Part 2

    Topics covered: Sampling and aliasing with a sinusoidal signal, sinusoidal response of a digital filter, dependence of frequency response on sampling period, periodic nature of the frequency response of a digital filter. Instructor: Prof. Alan V. Oppenheim

  4. Lecture 2: Discrete-Time Signals and Systems, Part 1

    Topics covered: Definitions of basic discrete-time signals: The unit sample, unit step, exponential and sinusoidal sequences, definitions and representations of linear time-invariant discrete-time systems, properties of discrete-time convolution. Instructor: P

  5. Lecture 3: Discrete-Time Signals and Systems, Part 2

    Topics covered: Stability and causality for discrete-time systems, systems described by linear constant-coefficient difference equations, frequency response of linear time-invariant systems. Instructor: Prof. Alan V. Oppenheim

  6. Lecture 4: The Discrete-Time Fourier Transform

    Topics covered: Generalization of the frequency response representation of sequences, inverse Fourier transform relation, symmetry properties of Fourier transforms, relationship between continuous-time and discrete-time Fourier transforms. Instructor: Prof. Al

  7. Lecture 5: The z-Transform

    Topics covered: Generalization of Fourier transform to z-transform, relationship of Fourier transform to z-transform, region of convergence of z transforms, characteristics of region of convergence in relation to poles, zeros, stability, and causality. Instruc

  8. Lecture 6: The Inverse z-Transform

    Topics covered: Methods of implementing the inverse z-transforms: Inspection method, power series, partial fraction expansion, and contour integration. Instructor: Prof. Alan V. Oppenheim

  9. Lecture 7: z-Transform Properties

    Topics covered: Geometric determination of frequency response from pole-zero patterns in the z-plane, properties of z-transforms: Scaling, differentiation, shifting, and convolution, examples of proof of properties of z-transforms. Instructor: Prof. Alan V. Op

  10. Lecture 8: The Discrete Fourier Series

    Topics covered: Fourier series representation for periodic sequences, determination of Fourier series coefficients, properties of Fourier series. Instructor: Prof. Alan V. Oppenheim

  11. Lecture 9: The Discrete Fourier Transform

    Topics covered: Sampling and aliasing with a sinusoidal signal, sinusoidal response of a digital filter, dependence of frequency response on sampling period, periodic nature of the frequency response of a digital filter. Instructor: Prof. Alan V. Oppenheim

  12. Lecture 10: Circular Convolution

    Topics covered: Circular convolution of finite length sequences, interpretation of circular convolution as linear convolution followed by aliasing, implementing linear convolution by means of circular convolution. Instructor: Prof. Alan V. Oppenheim

  13. Lecture 11: Representation of Linear Digital Networks

    Topics covered: Block diagram presentation of difference equations, linear-signal flow graphs, flow graph representation of difference equations, matrix representation of digital networks, computability of digital networks. Instructor: Prof. Alan V. Oppenheim

  14. Lecture 12: Network Structures for Infinite Impulse Response (IIR) Systems

    Topics covered: Basic network structures for IIR filters, direct cascade and parallel form, canonic structures, transposition theorem for digital networks and the resulting transposed forms. Instructor: Prof. Alan V. Oppenheim

  15. Lecture 13: Network Structures for Finite Impulse Response (FIR) Systems and Parameter Quantization Effects in Digital Filter Structures

    Topics covered: Direct form FIR filters, efficient implementation of FIR filters with linear phase, frequency sampling structure, effects of parameter-quantization in digital filter implementation. Instructor: Prof. Alan V. Oppenheim

  16. Lecture 14: Design of IIR Digital Filters, Part 1

    Topics covered: Transformation of analog filter designs to digital filter designs, approximation of derivatives by differences, impulse invariant design procedures. Instructor: Prof. Alan V. Oppenheim

  17. Lecture 15: Design of IIR Digital Filters, Part 2

    Topics covered: Digital filter design using the bilinear transformation, frequency warping introduced by the bilinear transformation, algorithmic design procedures for IIR filters. Instructor: Prof. Alan V. Oppenheim

  18. Lecture 16: Digital Butterworth Filters

    Topics covered: Design of digital Butterworth filter using impulse invariance, design of digital Butterworth filter using the bilinear transformation, comparison of the resulting designs. Instructor: Prof. Alan V. Oppenheim

  19. Lecture 17: Design of FIR Digital Filters

    Topics covered: Design of FIR filters using windows, comparison of rectangular, Bartlett, and Hamming windows, frequency sampling method of filter design, optimum equi-ripple FIR filters. Instructor: Prof. Alan V. Oppenheim

  20. Lecture 18: Computation of the Discrete Fourier Transform, Part 1

    Topics covered: Direct computation of the discrete-time Fourier transform, computation resulting from successive decimation of the sequences, the decimation-in-time form of the fast Fourier transform (FFT) algorithm, basic butterfly computation. Instructor: Pr

  21. Lecture 19: Computation of the Discrete Fourier Transform, Part 2

    Topics covered: Interpretation of FFT flow graph for in-place computation, bit-reversed data ordering, other decimation-in-time FFT algorithms by rearrangement of the flow graph, decimation-in-frequency FFT algorithm. Instructor: Prof. Alan V. Oppenheim

  22. Lecture 20: Computation of the Discrete Fourier Transform, Part 3

    Topics covered: Rearrangements of the basic decimation-in-frequency algorithm, relation between decimation-in-time and decimation-in-frequency through the transposition theorem, arbitrary radix FFT algorithms. Instructor: Prof. Alan V. Oppenheim
