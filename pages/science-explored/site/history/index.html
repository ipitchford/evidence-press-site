A connected history of science and technology

Explore the history of science and technology from the earliest surviving tools to November 2026. Each period pairs historical questions with museum objects, university teaching or observational records.

Explore by period

  1. Before 3000 BCE · What survives of a technology? Making and remembering
  2. 3000–500 BCE · How can an institution make a quantity travel? Counting, cultivating and building
  3. 500 BCE–600 CE · When does a repeated observation become a calculation? Arguments, instruments and organised knowledge
  4. 600–1450 · What changes when knowledge crosses languages? Knowledge across languages and trading worlds
  5. 1450–1650 · How did print and instruments change who could check a claim? Print, navigation and new observations
  6. 1650–1800 · What makes a measurement or machine work somewhere else? Experiment, calculation and useful work
  7. 1800–1870 · How could an instrument or a table expose a hidden regularity? Industrial systems and living processes
  8. 1870–1914 · What evidence could connect an invisible process to a visible effect? Networks, laboratories and invisible agents
  9. 1914–1945 · How can a trace stand in for an event too small or fast to see? New scales of matter and intervention
  10. 1945–1980 · How did researchers learn to read a planet or a molecule through instruments? Planetary instruments and molecular tools
  11. 1980–2009 · When can a network or a global record be trusted? Digital networks and changing environments
  12. 2010–November 2026 · How far can a striking result be generalised? Connected tools and unfinished questions
