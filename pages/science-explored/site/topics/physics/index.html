Topic guide

What can a redshift tell us about motion?

Follow the change in spacing between successive wave crests as a source recedes. The period measured by the observer is the time between arrivals. Which clock supplies that interval?

Topic guide

Can two processes transfer different amounts of heat between the same states?

Listen for how a boundary lets energy cross as heat or work. A state measurement can constrain their combined effect while leaving the history of the process open.

↗

Topic guide

Why does an isolated system approach equilibrium?

The entropy-maximisation argument requires a boundary condition. Track what can move between subsystems, what remains fixed for the whole, and where the extremum is taken.

↗

Topic guide

How does a solar cell turn light into current?

In the photoconductivity tutorial, separate the creation of mobile charge carriers from their movement. An increase in conductivity does not by itself specify which terminal will collect the electrons.

Topic guide

Where did the elements in a star come from?

Start by separating a census of elements from an explanation of their origin. The abundance of an element in a star is an observation that a history of production, mixing and incorporation must explain.

↗

Topic guide

What evidence could identify an early episode of chemical enrichment?

Consider how a first generation of stars can leave traces in material that formed later. The chemical pattern of a surviving star integrates events before its birth.

↗

Topic guide

Where does a lens form an image?

Trace a small group of rays from one object point. The paraxial approximation makes an off-axis ray easier to predict near the optical axis. Ask which rays you would inspect first to find the approximation breaking down.

↗

Topic guide

Why can two observers disagree about simultaneity?

Separate an event from the time a signal from that event reaches an observer. The history of special relativity asks what measurements would justify assigning a common time to spatially separated events.

↗

Topic guide

When does a force change angular momentum?

Begin with the free-body diagram and identify which object each force acts upon. A force can change linear momentum while its torque about a chosen point is zero.

↗

Topic guide

How can a conservation law survive a change of coordinates?

A vector and its components play different roles when a coordinate basis changes. Follow the basis through the introduction of four-vectors, then ask which physical statement both descriptions must preserve.

↗

Topic guide

How can resonance help measure a frequency?

Begin with the classical resonance of a driven oscillator. The natural frequency, the driving frequency and the width of the response peak describe different features of the experiment.
