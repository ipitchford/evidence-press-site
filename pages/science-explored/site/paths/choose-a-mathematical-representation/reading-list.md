# Choose a mathematical representation

For students preparing for proof-based courses. Decide what information is preserved by a sample space, a factorisation and a metric.

1. What must a probability model count as an outcome?
   - [01.2 Sample Space](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/res-6-012-introduction-to-probability-spring-2018/resources/sample-space/)
   - [01.4 Probability Axioms](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/res-6-012-introduction-to-probability-spring-2018/resources/probability-axioms/)
   List outcomes under the ordered-pair and head-count descriptions. Find one question both models can answer and one only the ordered model can answer. Assign probabilities and compare the result for exactly one head.
2. What does a matrix factorisation reveal about dependent columns?
   - [Elimination and Factorization A = CR](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/res-18-010-a-2020-vision-of-linear-algebra-spring-2020/resources/res-18010-strang-part-2-version-3_mp4/)
   - [Five Factorizations of a Matrix](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/res-18-010-a-2020-vision-of-linear-algebra-spring-2020/resources/five-factorizations-version-4_mp4/)
   Write a matrix whose third column is twice its first minus its second. Choose the independent columns and construct the coefficient matrix. Multiply back and check each column separately.
3. Why does compactness matter beyond ordinary Euclidean space?
   - [Lecture 3: Compact Sets in Rⁿ](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-s190-introduction-to-metric-spaces-january-iap-2023/resources/18s190-lecture-3/)
   - [Lecture 4: Compact Metric Spaces](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-s190-introduction-to-metric-spaces-january-iap-2023/resources/18s190-lecture-4/)
   In the discrete metric on the positive integers, find a radius below which each open ball contains just one point. Cover the whole space with such balls. Can a finite subcollection still cover it?
