Learning path

Following a movement system from muscle to brain

A learner seeking an ordered anatomy-and-control route rather than a general brain survey.

Work upwards through the dedicated human-movement course: motor units, spinal circuits and higher systems. The order preserves the relevant official course sequence while omitting unrelated lessons for a manageable first route. Each task adds to one labelled explanation of a hypothetical reach. Anatomical assignments remain tentative until supported; the aim is to connect levels, not memorise a different structure at every stop.

  1. · Neuroscience of Human Movement

    Enter through the dedicated course introduction and its stated scope.

    Write the movement you want to explain and list its sensory, muscular and timing requirements.

  2. · Neuroscience of Human Movement : Motor Units - Part 1

    Follow the official sequence from muscle foundations to motor units.

    Add a motor neuron and its supplied muscle fibres to your diagram; keep this separate from a whole-muscle control claim.

  3. · Neuroscience of Human Movement : Spine and Spinal Cord

    Locate spinal organisation before the reflex lesson.

    Mark where sensory information and motor output would enter your diagram, leaving uncertain pathways unlabelled.

  4. · Neuroscience of Human Movement : Monosynaptic Reflexes - Part 1

    Use the named monosynaptic-reflex lesson as a focused circuit question.

    Identify the minimum elements you would need to describe a sensory-to-motor circuit and a measurable response.

  5. · Neuroscience of Human Movement : Overview of motor control system

    Reassemble the parts in the course’s motor-control overview.

    Make a distinction between information available before movement and information available only after movement begins.

  6. · Neuroscience of Human Movement : Primary Motor Cortex Part - 2

    Extend the overview into the explicitly named primary-motor-cortex sequence.

    Ask what evidence would support a proposed contribution of cortex; distinguish recording from intervention.

  7. · Neuroscience of Human Movement : Cerebellum Part - 1

    Continue into the course’s cerebellum block.

    Add a question about this system’s contribution, recording the source claim separately from your inference.

  8. · Neuroscience of Human Movement : Basal Ganglia - Part 1

    Compare another higher system instead of assigning all control to one region.

    Complete a table of structures, proposed contributions and evidence needed. Mark any unanswered question rather than filling it with a familiar slogan.
