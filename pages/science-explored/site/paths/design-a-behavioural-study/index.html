Learning path

Design and evaluate a behavioural study

A beginning student planning a study on paper before collecting data.

An eight-step route from a population and outcome to a comparison, power assumptions and an interpretable interval. The associated guide and design readings supply the experimental-design concepts that the statistics demonstrations do not cover.

  1. · Lecture 06 : Objectives, Steps and Basic Definitions

    Begin by separating the people observed from the population the eventual claim concerns.

    Write a target population and a feasible sample; state one limitation in moving from one to the other.

  2. · Lecture 07 : Variables and Types of Data

    Turn the question into an observation before choosing a statistic.

    Define the primary outcome, units, time window and variable type.

  3. · Motivation

    With an outcome defined, narrow the scientific question to a quantity that a statistical comparison can address.

    State the target difference or proportion in ordinary language and explain what it leaves unanswered.

  4. · Statistics for Variance and Proportion

    Compare possible target quantities instead of assuming every study concerns an average.

    Explain why a mean, variability or proportion is the appropriate target for your particular question.

  5. · Hypothesis testing of means

    Now connect the comparison with the relation between observations.

    Sketch a separate-group and a paired version; read the linked design chapter and identify a carryover issue.

  6. · Power of hypothesis test

    Choose the effect you would want a planned test to detect.

    Name a meaningful alternative and distinguish it from the null hypothesis.

  7. · Factors affecting hypothesis test

    Stress-test the planning inputs rather than reporting one sample-size number.

    Describe how your plan would change under greater variability or a smaller relevant effect.

  8. · Confidence interval construction

    Finish with the estimate you would report and the uncertainty that should accompany it.

    Write a mock results sentence with placeholders for an effect estimate and confidence interval, then one sentence about a remaining design limitation.
