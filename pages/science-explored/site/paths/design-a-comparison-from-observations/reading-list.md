# Design a comparison from observations

For learners planning a small field study. Define the outcome, devise a sampling plan and distinguish competing types of explanation.

1. What must a probability model count as an outcome?
   - [01.2 Sample Space](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/res-6-012-introduction-to-probability-spring-2018/resources/sample-space/)
   - [01.4 Probability Axioms](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/res-6-012-introduction-to-probability-spring-2018/resources/probability-axioms/)
   List outcomes under the ordered-pair and head-count descriptions. Find one question both models can answer and one only the ordered model can answer. Assign probabilities and compare the result for exactly one head.
2. Which data can tell a transit planner why a route looks empty?
   - [Lecture 2: Data Collection Techniques and Program Design](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/1-258j-public-transportation-systems-spring-2017/resources/lecture-2-data-collection-techniques-and-program-design/)
   - [Lecture 3: Modal Characteristics and Roles](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/1-258j-public-transportation-systems-spring-2017/resources/lecture-3-modal-characteristics-and-roles/)
   Design a one-day survey to distinguish a lightly used route from a crowded route with long gaps between vehicles. Specify where and when to count, and identify a journey the survey might miss.
3. What kinds of explanation can answer one question about animal behaviour?
   - [Lec2: Introduction to ethology](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/9-20-animal-behavior-fall-2013/resources/mit9_20f13_lec02_mp4/)
   - [Lec4: Ethology and Konrad Lorenz' Jackdaws](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/9-20-animal-behavior-fall-2013/resources/mit9_20f13_lec04_mp4/)
   For a territorial display, write questions about immediate cause, development, evolutionary history and function. Pair each question with an observation that could answer it.
