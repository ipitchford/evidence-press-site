Learning path

Comparing minds across species without a single ladder

Learners who want to compare brains and behaviour while keeping the task, species and explanatory level explicit.

Move from scaling and task design to development, field behaviour and human origins. Each step changes one part of the comparison so that the final synthesis is a research plan, not a ranking of species.

  1. · Suzana Herculano-Houzel -- Scaling and Sizes

    Define what a brain comparison is meant to explain before choosing a measurement.

    Choose a hypothetical ability and list three candidate measurements. Explain what each would leave unmeasured.

  2. · Power Laws, Scale Invariance, and Diversity

    Add proportional reasoning to the initial measurement question.

    Use invented size values 1, 2 and 4 with a second measurement 1, 4 and 16. Calculate the changing ratio and label the exercise fictional; explain why it says nothing about real intelligence.

  3. · Neural Underpinnings of Individual Recognition in the Brain

    Turn from broad anatomical differences to a concrete recognition problem.

    Distinguish recognising an individual from responding to a familiar signal. Propose one comparison that would help separate those interpretations.

  4. · Delayed neuronal maturation in humans

    Add developmental timing and molecular questions without losing the behavioural target.

    Make separate boxes for developmental pattern, candidate molecular mechanism and proposed consequence. Mark which link the source identifies as unresolved.

  5. · Love, monogamy, and fatherhood in Latin American monkeys

    Move from a laboratory question to long-term field comparisons and competing explanations.

    List two explanations of pair bonding or care mentioned in the abstract. State an observation that would be more expected under one than the other.

  6. · Digging with documents: Ethnology as a tool for understanding human evolution

    Ask how present comparisons might inform a claim about human origins.

    Write one potential use and one limitation of a cross-cultural analogy. Identify what historical evidence would still be required.

  7. · Social complexity: Why modern humans are more like certain ants than chimpanzees

    Finish with a deliberately challenging analogy across very different organisms.

    Construct a one-page comparison plan with separate rows for anatomy, behaviour and organisation. Use the methods reading to identify a confound and an alternative explanation; avoid an overall species score.
