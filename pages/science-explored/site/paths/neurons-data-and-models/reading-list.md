# From neurons and spike data to network models

1. [1. Overview and Ionic Currents](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/9-40-introduction-to-neural-computation-spring-2018/resources/overview-and-ionic-currents/) — List the ingredients in the opening model and one observation each is intended to help explain.
2. [2. RC Circuit and Nernst Potential](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/9-40-introduction-to-neural-computation-spring-2018/resources/2/) — Draw the correspondence between a component of the model and the feature it represents. Mark one simplification explicitly.
3. [3. Nernst Potential and Integrate and Fire Models](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/9-40-introduction-to-neural-computation-spring-2018/resources/3/) — State what event counts as a model spike and one biological feature the representation does not attempt to reproduce.
4. [7. Synapses](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/9-40-introduction-to-neural-computation-spring-2018/resources/7/) — Compare two model situations that differ in synaptic input. Predict a difference and state the assumption behind it.
5. [8. Spike Trains](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/9-40-introduction-to-neural-computation-spring-2018/resources/8/) — Specify a data summary and a question it could answer. Describe information that the summary might discard.
6. [9. Receptive Fields](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/9-40-introduction-to-neural-computation-spring-2018/resources/9/) — Identify the stimulus and response variables in a receptive-field model. Propose a new stimulus that would test its limits.
7. [18. Recurrent Networks](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/9-40-introduction-to-neural-computation-spring-2018/resources/18/) — Write separately what the network is designed to do and what evidence would be required to treat it as an explanation of a biological system.
8. [20. Hopfield Networks](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/9-40-introduction-to-neural-computation-spring-2018/resources/20/) — State what the model counts as successful storage or retrieval. Identify a failure condition and explain why it is informative rather than merely inconvenient.
