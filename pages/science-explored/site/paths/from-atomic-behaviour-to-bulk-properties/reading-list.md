# From atomic behaviour to bulk properties

For materials students deciding which physical scale explains a measurement. Move from an electron, to a semiconductor, to the walls of a structured solid.

1. What does a two-slit pattern tell us about an electron?
   - [Lecture 2: Wave Nature of the Electron and the Internal Structure of an Atom](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/5-61-physical-chemistry-fall-2017/resources/wave-nature-of-the-electron-and-the-internal-structure-of-an-atom/)
   - [Lecture 3: Two-Slit Experiment, Quantum Weirdness](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/5-61-physical-chemistry-fall-2017/resources/two-slit-experiment-quantum-weirdness/)
   Sketch the intensity you expect from two independent single-slit distributions added together. On the same axes draw a pattern with interference minima. Identify the observation that distinguishes the predictions.
2. How does a solar cell turn light into current?
   - [Tutorial: Photoconductivity](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-627-fundamentals-of-photovoltaics-fall-2013/resources/tutorial-photoconductivity/)
   - [Tutorial: Solar Cell Operation](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-627-fundamentals-of-photovoltaics-fall-2013/resources/tutorial-solar-cell-operation/)
   Draw an illuminated piece of silicon with no terminals attached. Add a junction and contacts in a second sketch. Mark which arrows describe the creation of carriers and which describe their separation or collection.
3. Why can honeycombs made of the same material have different stiffnesses?
   - [Lecture 3: Structure of Cellular Solids](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/3-054-cellular-solids-structure-properties-and-applications-spring-2015/resources/lecture-3-structure-of-cellular-solids/)
   - [Lecture 4: Honeycombs: In-plane Behavior](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/3-054-cellular-solids-structure-properties-and-applications-spring-2015/resources/lecture-4-honeycombs-in-plane-behavior/)
   Draw one cell and an in-plane load. Mark where you expect bending, then rotate the load. State which dimensions must stay fixed in a fair comparison of the two arrangements.
