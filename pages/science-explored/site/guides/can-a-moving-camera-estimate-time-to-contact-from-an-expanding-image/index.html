Topic guide

Can a moving camera estimate time to contact from an expanding image?

Start with Lecture 2: Image Formation, Perspective Projection, Time Derivative, Motion Field

Follow the projection of an approaching object onto the image plane. A single apparent size confounds size and distance. Its rate of change supplies another piece of evidence under assumptions about motion.

Try it

Measure the apparent width of an object in three successive sketches. State the assumptions needed to infer an approach time. Describe a rotation or change in physical size that would fool that inference.

Check your reasoning

For a fixed-width object approaching head-on at constant speed, apparent width divided by its rate of increase estimates time remaining. Slowly changing image size puts a small, noisy quantity in the denominator. Changes in orientation can also imitate approach.

Continue with Lecture 3: Time to Contact, Focus of Expansion, Direct Motion Vision Methods, Noise Gain

The next machine-vision lecture treats the inverse problem and noise gain. Ask which measured quantity becomes unstable when expansion is very small.
