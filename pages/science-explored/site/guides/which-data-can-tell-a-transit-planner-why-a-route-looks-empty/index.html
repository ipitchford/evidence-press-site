Topic guide

Which data can tell a transit planner why a route looks empty?

Start with Lecture 2: Data Collection Techniques and Program Design

Count passengers at one stop, on one vehicle and over an entire day. Each count has a different denominator. A quiet observation can reflect timing or sampling as easily as demand.

Try it

Design a one-day survey to distinguish a lightly used route from a crowded route with long gaps between vehicles. Specify where and when to count, and identify a journey the survey might miss.

Check your reasoning

A vehicle count measures supply, whereas counts of boarding passengers measure only trips that were actually made. Neither directly records travellers who gave up. Observing both vehicle headways and loads helps separate sparse demand from irregular service.

Continue with Lecture 3: Modal Characteristics and Roles

The lecture on modal characteristics asks how the vehicle and service pattern shape the meaning of the observations.
