Topic guide

How can layers of rock record the direction of an ancient current?

Start with Lecture 3: Bed Forms

Begin with a moving bedform. A dune has a shape at an instant and a history of migration. The layers it leaves behind need not preserve the whole shape.

Try it

Draw three overlapping positions of a migrating dune. Shade what a later dune removes. From the surviving inclined layers, infer one direction and identify one ambiguity.

Check your reasoning

The preserved inclinations can record deposition on the downstream face of a migrating form. Erosion removes some of the record. A single cut through a three-dimensional structure may show an apparent dip smaller than the true dip.

Continue with Lecture 4: Sedimentary Structures Produced by Sediment Transport

The lecture on sedimentary structures follows migrating ripples and dunes into the rock record. Compare its erosion surfaces with the ones in your drawing.
