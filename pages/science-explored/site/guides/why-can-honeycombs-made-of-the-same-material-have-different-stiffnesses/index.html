Topic guide

Why can honeycombs made of the same material have different stiffnesses?

Start with Lecture 3: Structure of Cellular Solids

Look at the direction and thickness of the cell walls. An applied force can stretch one wall and bend another, even when both walls are made of the same solid.

Try it

Draw one cell and an in-plane load. Mark where you expect bending, then rotate the load. State which dimensions must stay fixed in a fair comparison of the two arrangements.

Check your reasoning

Slender walls offer much less resistance to bending than to axial stretching. Changing the loading direction can therefore change the effective stiffness without changing the solid itself. Relative density and boundary conditions also affect the comparison.

Continue with Lecture 4: Honeycombs: In-plane Behavior

The in-plane behaviour lecture connects the deformation pattern to mechanical properties. Look for a prediction that depends on geometry.
