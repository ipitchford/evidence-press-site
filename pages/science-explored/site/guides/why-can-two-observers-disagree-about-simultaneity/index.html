Topic guide

Why can two observers disagree about simultaneity?

Start with Lecture 1.4: Space, Time, and Spacetime

Separate an event from the time a signal from that event reaches an observer. The history of special relativity asks what measurements would justify assigning a common time to spatially separated events.

Try it

Draw two lightning flashes, the light paths to a midpoint and the path of a moving observer. Label emission, reception and the inferred times. Identify the synchronisation assumption behind the inference.

Check your reasoning

A midpoint observer at rest relative to the flashes receives simultaneously emitted light together. Assigning emission times in a moving frame also requires a rule for synchronising separated clocks. Reception order alone does not supply that rule.

Continue with Lecture 3: The Doppler Effect and Special Relativity

The Doppler and relativity lecture puts clock readings and signal arrival intervals into a quantitative treatment.
