Topic guide

Where does a lens form an image?

Start with Lecture 3: Focusing, imaging, and the paraxial approximation

Trace a small group of rays from one object point. The paraxial approximation makes an off-axis ray easier to predict near the optical axis. Ask which rays you would inspect first to find the approximation breaking down.

Try it

Draw rays from two object points through a converging lens. Move the object inside the focal length, extend the outgoing rays backwards and record where their extensions meet.

Check your reasoning

Inside the focal length, outgoing rays from an object point can diverge while their backward extensions meet. A screen on the image side cannot capture that virtual image directly. Changing the object position tests the prediction.

Continue with Lecture 4: Sign conventions; thin lenses; real and virtual images

The thin-lens lecture introduces the sign conventions that distinguish an image formed by intersecting rays from one located by their backward extensions.
