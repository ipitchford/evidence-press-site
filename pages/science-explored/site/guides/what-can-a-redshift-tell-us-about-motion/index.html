Topic guide

What can a redshift tell us about motion?

Start with Lecture 3: The Doppler Effect and Special Relativity

Follow the change in spacing between successive wave crests as a source recedes. The period measured by the observer is the time between arrivals. Which clock supplies that interval?

Try it

Draw four emitted crests at equal source-time intervals. Increase the distance travelled by each successive crest and mark their arrivals. Identify the assumption that makes this a model of relative motion.

Check your reasoning

For a receding emitter the later crests travel farther, so the arrival interval grows. To convert a measured shift into a velocity you need the unshifted wavelength and a model relating wavelength to motion. On cosmological scales the expansion history enters that model.

Continue with Lecture 4: The Kinematics of the Homogeneous Expanding Universe

The next lecture introduces the relation between distance and recession in a homogeneous expanding universe. Ask where the moving-source analogy ceases to explain the geometry.
