Learning path

Development from behaviour to brain and study design

Compare two introductory accounts of child development, extend the question across adulthood, then move to cortical organisation and the setting in which developmental studies are conducted. The recurring task is to separate a target ability from the demands of its measurement.

For: Readers beginning developmental science who want to connect child and adult behaviour with questions about brain organisation and measurement.

What you will work towards

  • Describe a developmental question without treating a historical theory as an individual timetable.
  • Compare behavioural and brain-level observations of change.
  • Identify task demands and recruitment features that affect interpretation.
  • Propose an additional comparison that would distinguish explanations of a developmental difference.

A short starting route

Steps 1, 2, 3. The first three give two perspectives on childhood and an explicit extension into adulthood before introducing brain organisation.

Continue more deeply

Use the developmental guide’s paper exercise to compare two measures of one ability, then consult the Human Brain reading list for named studies behind its development lectures. Continue at source ↗

Limits of this route

The introductory courses are from 2007 and 2011; the Human Brain collection is from 2019, its second development video is labelled 2018, and the Lookit tutorial is dated 2020. Descriptions and selected teaching pages were inspected, not full videos or the current platform. This is an educational route, not an assessment of individual development or current clinical guidance.
