Learning path

From neurons and spike data to network models

Begin with physical and circuit models, connect them to synapses and spike data, then extend to receptive fields and recurrent memory models. Each step asks what the representation includes and what observation would challenge it. This is a selected route, with the full course available for omitted derivations.

For: Mathematically prepared learners entering computational neuroscience who want a selected route through MIT’s full lecture sequence.

What you will work towards

  • Identify assumptions and omissions in a neuron model.
  • Relate spike-data summaries to a stated modelling question.
  • Describe how response and recurrent-network models address different problems.
  • Separate fitting or generating a behaviour from validating a biological explanation.

Steps 1, 2, 3. The first three lectures form a coherent physical-to-circuit-to-neuron-model entry before the route branches into data and networks.

Continue more deeply

Return to the full MIT neural-computation course for the intervening conductance, signal-analysis and linear-algebra lectures. Use Neuromatch’s linear-regression tutorial for an inspected Python-based fitting exercise. Continue at source ↗

Limits of this route

The MIT lecture sequence is from 2018. Descriptions, the course’s assignment listing and selected Neuromatch explanatory text were inspected; full videos, all derivations and executable assignments were not. Intermediate course lectures are omitted deliberately, so the listed mathematical prerequisites matter. A model’s successful behaviour does not alone establish biological validity.
