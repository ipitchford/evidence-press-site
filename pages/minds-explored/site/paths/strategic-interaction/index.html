Learning path

A first route through strategic interaction

Preserves order within Yale’s course while selecting a bridge from simultaneous choice to timing, information and repetition.

For: Learners prepared to work through examples in introductory game theory.

What you will work towards

  • Identify players, actions, outcomes and payoffs separately.
  • Find best responses and explain why equilibrium need not be desirable.
  • Show how timing, information and repetition alter the model of a choice.

Before you start

  • Yale states introductory microeconomics as a prerequisite, uses mainly one-variable calculus and refers to probability and expectation.
  • A small payoff-table exercise can be attempted first, but the full route is mathematically more demanding than a general-interest talk.
