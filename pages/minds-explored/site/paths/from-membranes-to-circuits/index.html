Learning path

From membranes to interacting circuits

This route follows the mechanism of communication: charge movement, membrane response, transmission and interacting populations. It differs from a general introduction to computational neuroscience by organising every step around what changes between an input and an output. Begin with conceptual sketches, then use the mathematical and notebook material only as preparation allows. The recurring task is to identify what is measured, what is modelled and what the model omits.

For: A learner who wants a physical and biological route from cells to small circuit models.

What you will work towards

  • Explain why concentration and voltage both matter for ionic current.
  • Distinguish an observed signal from a reduced model of it.
  • Separate transmission, short-term synaptic dynamics and a plasticity rule.
  • Describe what a population model adds to a single-cell explanation.

Limits of this route

The route combines a 2011 introductory transcript opening, 2018 MIT descriptions and inspected Neuromatch tutorial sections. No notebook was executed and no whole-video viewing is claimed. The model sequence is an editorial teaching route, not independent validation of the biological completeness of any model.

Before you start

  • For a conceptual skim, use the introductory explanation and descriptive parts of steps 1–4 without attempting derivations. Mathematical study of the ionic/RC and reduced-neuron steps requires algebra, calculus and elementary differential equations. The Wilson–Cowan continuation requires calculus and phase-plane reasoning, with linear algebra for Jacobian/eigenvalue stability work; executing the notebooks requires Python.
