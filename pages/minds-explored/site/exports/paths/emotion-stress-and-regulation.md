# Emotion, stress and questions about regulation

Begin with what an emotional episode contains, compare functional and social explanations, and then study stress and regulation as research questions. The route repeatedly asks what was measured and what would count as a change. It does not promise improved wellbeing or teach a treatment package. The final task turns an appealing regulation claim into an evaluable study question.

1. [Lecture 15: Emotion & Motivation](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/9-00sc-introduction-to-psychology-fall-2011/resources/lecture-15-emotion-and-motivation/)
   Establish the components of emotion before comparing explanations.
   Task: Describe one fictional episode using separate labels for feeling, activation, bodily change and action.

2. [Lecture 11 - Evolution, Emotion, and Reason: Emotions, Part I](https://oyc.yale.edu/psychology/psyc-110/lecture-11)
   Add an evolutionary perspective to the same explanatory problem.
   Task: Identify one proposed function and write what evidence would be needed beyond its apparent plausibility.

3. [Lecture 12 - Evolution, Emotion, and Reason: Emotions, Part II](https://oyc.yale.edu/psychology/psyc-110/lecture-12)
   Extend the comparison to social relationships and reciprocity.
   Task: Ask whether the proposed explanation concerns the individual, an interaction or a broader social pattern.

4. [Lec 3: Communication and Measurement of Emotions](https://nptel.ac.in/courses/109103395)
   Make measurement explicit before moving to regulation claims.
   Task: Choose a self-report, behavioural measure and physiological measure; state why they need not be identical observations.

5. [Lecture 19: Stress](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/9-00sc-introduction-to-psychology-fall-2011/resources/lecture-19-stress/)
   Shift from an emotion episode to demands, anticipation and time.
   Task: Distinguish a stressor, an appraisal or response, and a health outcome in a hypothetical headline.

6. [Lec 3 : The Biology of Stress](https://nptel.ac.in/courses/109103182)
   Add the stress course’s named biological component.
   Task: Record a proposed biological link as a hypothesis and list the observation that would test it.

7. [Lec 20: Emotion regulation: Introduction](https://nptel.ac.in/courses/109103395)
   Enter the emotion course’s explicitly named introduction to regulation.
   Task: Define what is intended to change: a report, behaviour, duration or other outcome. Do not assume all improve together.

8. [Lec 9 : Factors Influencing Stress Tolerance](https://nptel.ac.in/courses/109103182)
   Finish with variation rather than a universal response recipe.
   Task: Design a paper-only comparison that specifies population, comparator, outcome and follow-up for a regulation claim.
