Topic guide

How is speech different from language?

Separate a communication medium from linguistic structure, meaning and context, then locate perception and production within that map.

For: Readers seeking a first conceptual map of language science.

A clear sound recording is not a complete record of what a sentence means. A listener also needs linguistic knowledge and, often, a sense of the situation. Conversely, the study of language is not confined to sound: Yale’s opening lecture explicitly frames its subject through the brain, mouth and hands. This route keeps the medium of expression distinct from the system being expressed.

Begin by separating several questions. How is a signal produced or perceived? How are meaningful units combined? How does a sentence acquire an interpretation in a conversation? MIT’s language session introduces phonology, syntax and semantics and places those topics beside acquisition and neural questions. These are related levels of explanation, not different names for one process.

The Psychology of Language course supplies a deliberate order: communication and language, the science of language, speech perception and speech production. Keep that order when it is useful. Perception begins with an incoming signal; production begins with something a speaker wants to express. Comparing them helps expose the temptation to assume that one is simply the other played backwards.

Yoshihisa Kashima’s language-use chapter adds the social situation. Conversation requires participants to coordinate what is being referred to and what is already shared. That is why studying a word list, a sentence and a conversation can answer different questions even when the same words appear in all three.

As you follow the selections, make a small table with columns for signal, structure, meaning and context. Put each claim in the appropriate column and note where it links two columns. A brain result, for example, must still specify the task and the comparison that produced it; naming a region does not replace a description of the linguistic operation.

The route offers a conceptual foundation rather than a diagnosis of speech or language difficulties. Its older introductory lectures are dated teaching resources. Continue to the word-recognition guide for a narrower processing question, or to acquisition if your main interest is how a learner develops this knowledge.
