Topic guide

How do listeners resolve sentence ambiguity?

Represent more than one interpretation of a sentence and distinguish that structural problem from explaining comprehension over time.

For: Learners ready to compare linguistic structure with comprehension.

A sentence may use familiar words and still permit more than one interpretation. The problem is sometimes the relation between phrases, not a missing dictionary entry. This guide separates describing an ambiguity from explaining how a listener arrives at an interpretation as the sentence unfolds.

Start with the course’s “What is a sentence?” lecture and continue through the first three parsing lectures in their original order. The purpose is to keep a structural question beside a processing question. A description of the possible structures tells you what interpretations are available. A processing account must additionally explain what happens when, what information is used and which evidence would distinguish competing accounts.

The inspected structural-ambiguity chapter illustrates how an attached phrase can modify different parts of a sentence. Try representing the two interpretations before deciding which seems more natural. Familiarity with one interpretation is not evidence that the other structure is impossible.

Context adds another layer. The discourse selection and Noba’s discussion of language use direct attention to the information speakers and listeners share. A description that makes sense in one conversation may be odd in another. Distinguish that contextual preference from a grammatical impossibility.

The original exercise uses a short sentence about photographing a visitor. It is intentionally small enough to paraphrase in two ways. Add a context that favours each version, then ask what you would need to measure to study processing: a final interpretation, a response time, a reading pattern or something else. These measures would answer related but different questions.

This route does not infer a processing mechanism from a tree diagram, nor does it treat a single introspective judgement as a population result. The course selections were inspected at the level of their official outline and sequence; the reading’s worked example was inspected directly. A useful continuation is to compare spoken and written comprehension while keeping the structural ambiguity the same.

Give one sentence two structures

Write “I photographed the visitor with the red bag.” Draw or paraphrase two possible attachments of the final phrase. Add one context that favours each reading; explain why a structural description is not yet a processing experiment.
