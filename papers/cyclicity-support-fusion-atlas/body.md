# The all-degree low-rank coefficient atlas

This release moves the coefficient atlas from a finite degree-twenty theorem
candidate to an **all-degree written classification over the complex numbers**,
relative to named results on primitive polynomial monodromy and finite
projective linear groups. It also classifies every proper polynomial outer
extension that preserves fixed-seed cyclic rank at most three.

That is the scientific advance. The assurance boundary is equally important:
the result is anonymous, unrefereed, not completely formalised, and not
independently reproduced. The imported classification theorems are cited and
audited, not reproved.

## The classification in one table

Let $q(P)$ be the cyclic rank generated by $[dx]$ for a complex polynomial
phase $P$. Up to invertible affine changes of source and target, the candidate
classifies every phase with $q(P)\leq3$ as follows.

| Rank | Family | Scope condition |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | $x^d$ | $d\geq2$ |
| 2 | $D_d(x,a)$ | Dickson family, with its rank-one intersections removed |
| 2 | $x^{2m}+Ax^m$ | $m\geq2$, outside lower-rank loci |
| 3 | $x^{3m}+Ax^{2m}+Bx^m$ | $m\geq2$, outside the recorded rank-one and rank-two loci |
| 3 | $x^4+Ax^2+Bx$ | $B\neq0$ |
| 3 | $(x^4+ax)^3$ | $a\neq0$ |

For the cubic-over-power row, $A=B=0$ is rank one. Its exceptional rank-two
intersection occurs at $m=2$, $B=A^2/4$, $A\neq0$. The quartic row requires
$B\neq0$; its $B=0$ boundary belongs to lower rank. The statement classifies
affine families, not unique parameter coordinates.

The theorem is universal in degree, but conditional on the correctness and
applicability of the named imported classifications. That dependence is part
of the statement, not a footnote.

## The fixed-seed bridge

For a monic centred polynomial $P$ of degree $d$, let

$$
\xi(t)=t+O(t^{-1}),\qquad P(\xi(t))=t^d,
$$

and split the inverse series into residue channels

$$
X_r(t)=\frac1d\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\zeta_d^{-jr}\xi(\zeta_d^jt),
\qquad
\Sigma(P)=\{r\bmod d:X_r\neq0\}.
$$

Fourier inversion recovers the inverse branches from the $X_r$. Distinct
nonzero channels have disjoint Laurent supports, so they are linearly
independent. Combined with the inherited formal-moment argument, this gives

$$
\boxed{q(P)=|\Sigma(P)|=\dim_{\mathbf C}W_P^{\mathrm{red}}.}
$$

Here $W_P^{\mathrm{red}}$ is the reduced constant span of the generic inverse
roots. No contour comparison is needed for this algebraic identity. The
fixed-sector Fourier--Gamma period determinant remains a separate analytic
realisation.

If $P=R\circ Q$ and $m=\deg Q$, residue projection gives

$$
q(Q)=|\Sigma(P)\bmod m|\leq q(P).
$$

Equality means every generic $Q$-block spans the whole root space. That
full-fusion condition is the entry point for the extension classification.

## Four interfaces that close the all-degree proof

The finite atlas did not stop because degree thirty-two was intrinsically too
large. It stopped because several structural interfaces had not yet been
proved. Version 0.6 closes them.

### 1. Finite-pole and infinity-order descent

Let $y_1,\ldots,y_n$ be the roots of $A(Y)-z$ and choose radicals
$v_i^m=y_i-b$. For $f_k=e_k(v_1,\ldots,v_n)$, monodromy multiplies $f_k$ by
an $m$-th root of unity. Hence $f_k^m$ lies in $\mathbf C(z)$. Because the
roots and radicals are integral over $\mathbf C[z]$, so is $f_k^m$; normality
of $\mathbf C[z]$ forces $f_k^m\in\mathbf C[z]$.

At infinity its order is at most $k/n$. Therefore $f_k$ is constant for
$k<n$, while

$$
f_n^m=(-1)^n(A(b)-z).
$$

This algebraic invariant-integral argument supplies the coefficient descent
that an earlier route tried to obtain from analytic boundedness.

### 2. Primitive-factor audit

After removing the maximal power suffix, the next indecomposable factor has a
primitive monodromy group and its radical lines span a projective space of
dimension at most two. The proof intersects the complete primitive polynomial
monodromy list it imports with the finite primitive subgroups of
$\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbf C)$ and $\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbf C)$.

The remaining nonsolvable candidates are excluded by explicit stabiliser
character calculations. What survives is exactly a prime power, a prime
Dickson/Chebyshev polynomial, or a quartic with $S_4$ monodromy. The source
audit records every imported group, degree, stabiliser and theorem locator.

### 3. Full-block stabilisers

When $q(P)=q(Q)$, every $Q$-block spans $W_P$. An element of the block kernel
that fixes one block pointwise therefore fixes the entire root span and hence
every inverse root; faithfulness makes it the identity. More precisely, the
setwise stabiliser of a block surjects onto the full geometric monodromy of
$Q$ on that block.

This removes a dangerous shortcut. Equality of two spans does not by itself
identify their point configurations; the full stabiliser lemma is what
licenses the later normaliser arguments.

### 4. Kummer strata and mixed Dickson chains

Adversarial review found that a proposed full Kummer kernel is false when
$B\neq0$, $A^2=4B$, and the exponent is even. Version 0.6 preserves that
counterexample. Off the exceptional stratum, full-block stabilisers and the
normal diagonal subgroup force transport to permute the three Kummer weight
lines. On the exceptional stratum, the phase is already an aligned square of
a nonexceptional cubic-over-power phase, so a separate descent proves that no
further quotient exists.

For Dickson phases, the candidate avoids induction over a chosen prime-factor
chain. A block recovers a uniformiser $U$ and $a/U$. Rank preservation places
every inverse root in $\mathbf C(U)$, and the full Galois group normalises the
multiplicative torus. Its invariant field is generated by a single larger
Dickson polynomial. This proves the arbitrary-outer-polynomial result at once,
including mixed decomposition chains and all intervening Ritt moves.

## The complete extension oracle

The proper rank-preserving outer extensions are exactly

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{powers:}&x^m\longmapsto x^{mn},\\
\text{Dickson:}&D_m(x,a)\longmapsto D_{mn}(x,a),\\
\text{aligned square:}&x^{3m}+b x^m\longmapsto(x^{3m}+b x^m)^2,\\
\text{quartic collision:}&x^4+a x\longmapsto(x^4+a x)^3.
\end{array}
$$

The two rank-three mechanisms cannot repeat. This turns the classification
into an order oracle: follow a complete decomposition from the right, locate
the first rank increase, and then allow only the extension moves above.

The degree-twelve collision is no longer an isolated curiosity. It is the
unique quartic rank-preserving extension:

$$
(x^4+ax)^3.
$$

Its two decomposition views are reconciled by the same Fourier block-fusion
calculus that resolved the original collision.

## What computation contributes—and what it does not

The finite degree-twenty atlas remains intact: 1,152 exact-rational support
cases, comprising 45 full-family cases, 884 power-only certificates and 223
union certificates, with zero anomalies and zero missing certificates. The
preregistered degree-thirty-two experiment also passed its sealed protocol.

Those computations are now regression and bridge evidence. They check that
the universal structural theorem agrees with every bounded case already
computed. They do **not** prove the universal result by extrapolation.

The v0.6 gate adds:

- sixteen scoped Lean theorem controls;
- ten exact global checks in a separate producer-side CAS implementation;
- nineteen repaired Kummer checks;
- 2,079 pole-floor and 81 Dickson regression cases;
- thirteen adversarial semantic mutations, plus frozen acceptance, under
  ordinary and optimised Python;
- two separate internal model-assisted specialist re-audits after repair;
- clean-extraction replay and successful public Linux CI; and
- deterministic PDF, manifest and archive identities with byte-for-byte
  GitHub and Zenodo readback.

These controls can catch many implementation, receipt and presentation
failures. They cannot establish that every mathematical bridge is correct.

## A publication control added after visual QA

During release preparation, visual inspection caught display equations being
emitted as literal TeX even though ordinary text extraction did not flag the
failure. The v0.6 paper was rerendered with static KaTeX, inspected page by
page, and subjected to a fail-closed raw-TeX preflight. The preflight now has
a tested PyMuPDF fallback when `pdftotext` is unavailable, because public CI
exposed that portability gap.

This is a publication-quality control, not mathematical evidence. Its purpose
is narrower and essential: the reader-facing PDF must contain the same
legible equations that the proof source intends.

## What is imported

The load-bearing primitive and quartic-extension reductions use named results
from Behajaina, Koenig and Neftin on polynomial monodromy of composition
length two, together with finite subgroup classifications for
$\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbf C)$ and $\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbf C)$.
Burda and Khovanskii provide an independent source-to-list comparison, and
Zieve and Müller supply the Ritt-decomposition framework.

The release does not reprove those classifications or the source-side Magma
calculations. The all-degree theorem should therefore be read as **relative to
those named imports**.

## Assurance boundary

- No unaffiliated researcher has reported rerunning the immutable candidate.
- No independent team has reimplemented the classification or extension
  oracle.
- Lean checks selected local consequences, not the complete proof or imported
  classifications.
- The two specialist re-audits are internal and model-assisted.
- No journal or comparable venue has conducted editorial peer review.
- No absolute novelty, priority, adoption or field-impact claim is made.
- Positive characteristic, Laurent phases, zero cycles and arbitrary
  amplitudes remain outside the theorem.

The correct decision is: **GO for public specialist circulation as an
unrefereed theorem candidate; HOLD for promotion as an externally established
theorem.**
