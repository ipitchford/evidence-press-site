How complicated must the equations of a statistical model become? Moments describe features such as averages and higher powers. When variables are independent, their joint moments satisfy polynomial equations. The question is whether one fixed degree limit could always suffice to generate those equations, no matter how large the model becomes. This unrefereed candidate gives a constructive negative answer. For any proposed limit, its written proof builds a full moment model requiring a higher-degree equation. The key object is an even cycle. It has exactly two ways to pair all its vertices using disjoint edges. The construction turns those matchings into two products of moment coordinates with the same parameter image. They share no factor, so their difference cannot come from lower-degree equations. Crucially, the proof also prevents all the other model coordinates from supplying a shortcut. A supporting functional isolates the cycle as an exposed face. The same construction works for more general graphs and produces a separate geometric consequence: an intrinsically nonnormal moment variety. These are structural results. The number of variables and the moment order both grow, and the construction uses very large parameters. It does not settle the fixed-dimension question or show that every statistical algorithm is difficult. Readers can inspect the complete written argument and rerun two exact finite implementations.
