What happens to entropy when Gaussian noise smooths a probability distribution? The first change may be easy to predict. But a much stronger question asks whether every successive time derivative follows an alternating sign pattern.

This is an AI-assisted, unrefereed mathematical candidate from Evidence Press. It studies Tsallis entropy, a family indexed by a positive order parameter.

The result draws a sharp boundary. Only order two satisfies the entire derivative pattern universally. At every other positive order, counterexamples exist, even among smooth, strictly positive, strictly log-concave densities with Gaussian tails.

That sentence has an important qualification. For each fixed entropy order, the construction supplies a family of densities indexed by the derivative being tested. It is not one distribution that defeats every order at once.

The mechanism combines two periodic frequencies with a Gaussian envelope. Repeated differentiation amplifies a negative spectral contribution. This adapts earlier work; the Shannon counterexample, the localization method and quadratic positivity are explicitly attributed. The contribution is the full order classification, not the first refutation of the broader conjecture.

A separate example makes one failure concrete: entropy order three halves, derivative thirteen, and heat time equal to one divided by fifty. Exact rational arithmetic supports its finite comparisons. This particular witness is not claimed to be log-concave, or to fail at the earliest possible derivative.
