When an entanglement bound undercounts. An Evidence Press briefing.

Quantum entanglement can be hard to measure, especially when a system is mixed rather than perfectly isolated. One proposed shortcut uses a parallel swap test on two identical copies. The proposal treats the probability of certain outcomes as an upper bound on a quantity called concentratable entanglement.

This candidate shows exactly why that universal guarantee fails. Start with an entangled Bell pair and add an uncorrelated, maximally mixed qubit. The relevant entanglement remains one quarter. But the proposed upper bound drops to three sixteenths. The extra qubit changes which measurement outcomes are counted, without removing the Bell pair's entanglement.

The result goes beyond that simple example. The paper gives noisy states of full rank, a neighbourhood robust to arbitrary small perturbations, and states with genuine three-party entanglement. For the latter family, both ensemble optimisations are solved exactly.

It also supplies corrected bounds. A universal correction based on impurity has the best possible coefficient across all qubit counts. A sharper coefficient is proved optimal at each fixed size for the concave roof. The corresponding fixed-size convex-roof optimum remains open.

The original source already discussed possible underestimation. This work supplies explicit counterexamples and repairs, rather than claiming the concern was wholly unexpected.

The package contains proofs and exact computational checks. It remains an unrefereed, AI-assisted candidate, without external peer review or independent reproduction. This synthetic narration explains the result; it is not additional scientific evidence.
