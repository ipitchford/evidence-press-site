When researchers reconstruct ancient population sizes from modern genomes, they usually start from a simple histogram: how many genetic variants are rare, how many are common. This release proves exactly what that histogram can and cannot say. A sample of genomes delivers only a fixed, finite number of blurred averages over the past, and the paper characterises precisely which historical questions those averages answer - and which are silently filled in by the model. It also builds working machinery: certified bounds whose every endpoint carries an arithmetic certificate, checked by two independent mathematical routes. The machinery was then aimed, under a frozen, pre-registered protocol, at a live controversy: the claim that our ancestors crashed to about twelve hundred individuals nine hundred thousand years ago. The first stage found that no clean textbook model reproduces any of the nine published datasets if the data are treated as millions of independent observations. The second stage, under an honestly disclosed error model, certified a verdict on the claimants' own data: the ancient population was genuinely reduced in the disputed window - but nowhere near as severely as claimed. Two rival groups' versions of the same data agree. This is an unrefereed candidate: the full papers, code, protocols and certificates are linked, no independent group has reproduced the work, and this audio summary is not additional evidence.
