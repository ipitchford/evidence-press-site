A sequence of totals can look orderly while hiding different behaviour in the groups that make it up. This Evidence Press briefing concerns an unrefereed, AI-assisted mathematical candidate that keeps those groups visible.

A matroid describes which selections of objects are independent. Its inverse Z-polynomial packages information about that structure into a sequence of coefficients. When the matroid has symmetries, the coefficients can record representations: mathematical descriptions of the different ways those symmetries act.

Ordinary unimodality says that the total sizes rise towards the middle and then fall. The result here is stronger. Each individual symmetry type has its own symmetric, unimodal sequence of multiplicities.

The proof does not infer this from the totals. It lifts an already-public scalar identity into the representation ring. Truncation lowers the matroid's rank by one. Comparing the original matroid with its truncation expresses each increase, up to the middle, as a sum of two coefficients known to represent actual vector spaces with symmetry. An established positivity theorem supplies that last step.

A two-element example makes the distinction visible. One symmetry type occurs once in every degree; another occurs only in the middle. Their combined dimensions are one, two, one.

The package contains the written proof, precise source attribution and supplementary exact examples. The finite checks are not a proof of the general theorem. The resulting abstract symmetry action is noncanonical, and stronger log-concavity questions remain open. Internal AI review is not external peer review or formal verification.
