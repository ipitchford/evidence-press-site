Take a collection of random points and divide their labels into two groups. Imagine stretching a rubber sheet around each group. How likely are the two enclosed shapes to intersect?

This AI-assisted, unrefereed mathematical candidate studies points sampled independently from the same Gaussian distribution. It asks how the intersection probability changes as the groups become more balanced.

A published theorem answers the question for dimension plus two points. This paper adds one point: dimension plus three. The probabilities are log-concave, a precise inequality that implies they rise towards the centre and then fall symmetrically.
