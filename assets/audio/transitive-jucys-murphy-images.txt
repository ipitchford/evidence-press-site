This is an audio briefing from Evidence Press on a research candidate about shuffles, swaps and connected factorisations.

The symmetric group is the group of all shuffles of a set of objects. Its centre consists of the combinations of shuffles that do not depend on how the objects are labelled. Jucys–Murphy elements are particular sums of swaps, and plugging them into symmetric polynomials produces exactly this centre, a classical theorem of Jucys and of Murphy. Expanding gives products of swaps, whose counts are Hurwitz-type numbers that also count branched covers of the sphere.

Often only connected products matter: those whose swaps link all the objects together. Keeping only these gives the transitive image. Goulden and Jackson, and then Campion Loth and Rattan, showed that it is still central, and Campion Loth and Rattan asked which part of the centre these connected versions produce.

This candidate answers the question. Over the complex numbers, every central element is the connected version of a single symmetric function. Over a general number system, this works exactly when n minus one factorial can be divided by; otherwise even products of connected versions fall short. When it works, degree two n minus two is enough, and no smaller degree reaches the identity. Over the integers, an orbit-counting bound describes the image exactly for up to five objects and for seven, but not for six, where one extra parity condition appears.

The key idea is an inclusion–exclusion formula over ways of splitting the objects into groups, which turns each Jucys–Murphy element into a plain number, so the group sizes can be read off.

There are limits. The integer description is complete only up to seven objects, by exact computation, and the parity condition at six is not yet understood.

This is an unrefereed candidate, checked by internal AI-assisted review and exact computation rather than by peer review, and this briefing uses a synthetic voice that is not additional evidence.
