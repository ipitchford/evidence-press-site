Quantum transport cost cones: an Evidence Press briefing. Optimal transport looks for the cheapest way to move one distribution of mass onto another, and moving a unit of mass often costs a power of the distance travelled. With power one the cost is the distance itself; with power two it is the squared distance, which punishes long moves. In the quantum version of De Palma and Trevisan, transport between quantum states is carried out by a quantum channel, and a cost operator built from a measurable quantity plays the role of the powered distance. A state can have a positive cost of transport to itself, so these self-costs are subtracted to give a centred cost, and that centred cost is only guaranteed to be nonnegative for quadratic costs. Bunth, Pitrik, Titkos and Virosztek asked whether the collections of cost operators grow as the power increases. If they do, every cost with power up to two is secretly a sum of quadratic costs, and their quantum Wasserstein divergence is well defined. This candidate proves that they do grow, by carrying classical facts about the real line, due to Mendel and Naor and to Ball, into the quantum setting. Its main new tool is a retraction that replaces any cost by one built only from the given measurement blocks, without moving further from a block-shaped target. The consequence is that noncommuting observables never help. With this tool the paper computes exact errors. In at least three dimensions, the simplest limiting cost, which charges one unit for moving between two perpendicular states, is exactly one third away from all costs with power at most one, one fifth away when the power is two, and one seventeenth away when it is four, and no choice of measurements does better. Finally, for a single measurement with at least three outcomes, the centred cost never goes negative exactly when the power is at most two, and below that threshold a relaxed triangle inequality holds with a sharp constant. There are limits. The growth result concerns measurements with finitely many outcomes. For four or more levels only a lower bound is proved, and for small powers its exactness is only conjectured. Position and momentum are not covered, and whether sums of several noncommuting costs keep the centred cost nonnegative above power two is still open.
