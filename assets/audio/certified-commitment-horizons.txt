This anonymous, unrefereed research release asks a planning question: when demand forecasts change, how much movement can a production plan tolerate before its near-term setup decisions may need to change? The candidate studies this through a weighted commitment radius. Informally, the radius measures the nearest admissible forecast revision at which an optimal revised plan can stop sharing a protected prefix with the nominal plan. Version three makes an important repair. The software’s basic object is a forced-source path graph in which every complete path begins at period one. That graph does not always equal the ordinary physical lot-sizing model. If first-period demand can be zero, an ordinary plan may wait before opening a setup, while the forced-source graph still charges the first node. The release therefore uses a classical active-horizon interpretation only when the first-period demand floor is strictly positive and later setup costs are strictly positive, alongside the stated ordered-slope, inventory and no-backlogging assumptions. A leading-zero-demand counterexample is retained as a negative control. Within the declared model, the paper derives the exact floor-aware distance to a tie with any fixed competing path. It constructs a prefix-deviation graph, proves a polynomial exact-rational frozen-comparator lower certificate, and combines lower and upper witnesses into certified brackets. The familiar three-period example still has frozen radius three halves and exact strong radius two, showing that the lower certificate can be conservative. New structural results decompose a multi-arc protected prefix into one-arc suffix problems and show that every finite nearest loss has adjacent co-optimal inside and outside paths differing on a single divergence-and-remergence interval. The release also distinguishes exact values, certified intervals, guarded refusals and solver-level results instead of collapsing them into one answer. Its evidence includes ninety-four deterministic tests, three hundred twenty exact semantic-bridge comparisons, three hundred thirty brute-force certification comparisons, two hundred fifty-four finite optimal-face witnesses, exact-rational records, a separately implemented producer-side checker, fresh-extraction replay and CI on Python three point eleven and three point thirteen, both normally and with assertions disabled. The statistical layer offers one-step marginal conformal coverage only under exchangeability; it is not adaptive or time-uniform. The UCI case uses reconstructed forecast vintages and stylised costs, so it is an interface illustration, not evidence of industrial savings or field validity. GitHub and Zenodo expose matching public assets. Those facts establish availability and producer-side replay, not independent reproduction, formal verification, external specialist review, peer review, priority or impact. For reviewers, the highest-value next step is not another run of the same code. It is a clean-room reconstruction of the semantic bridge, fixed-path distance, prefix-deviation graph and single-detour argument, followed by an independent operations-research reading. A future operational study would need genuine forecast vintages, actual setup and holding costs, capacity and service constraints, a predeclared comparator, and outcomes collected prospectively. Until those gates are crossed, the release should be used as an auditable candidate method and research object, not as a validated production-planning policy.
