Entropy decay without a field ceiling. An Evidence Press briefing. Imagine a network of tiny magnets. Neighbours favour agreement, but each magnet also feels its own local field. A strong positive field prefers one direction; a strong negative field prefers the other. Can a random updating process approach equilibrium when these fields have no fixed upper bound? This mathematical candidate gives a continuous-time entropy-decay bound under a strong-disorder assumption: weak fields must be sufficiently uncommon, and the field magnitudes must be independent. Their signs may be arbitrary. The result also allows different magnitude distributions, provided they share the required bound on small-field probabilities. The proof adapts its local events to the preferred signs. Crucially, edges joining strong vertices with opposite preferences remain interacting. The endpoint is not a collection of independent spins. Rare minority spins instead form a low-activity system that can be controlled. A known warm-up method waits long enough for every site to have been updated. Combined with the entropy inequality, it removes field-tail dependence from the continuous-time mixing estimate on the same high-probability event. This is a structural result, not a practical runtime promise: the explicit constants and exponent can be very large. It does not establish the stronger one-step entropy contraction or approximate tensorization. The package contains a written proof and finite checks of selected ingredients, not a computer proof of the universal theorem.
