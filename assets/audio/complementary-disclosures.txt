Useful together. Not alone. An Evidence Press briefing.

Suppose you know how much each bank owes and is owed, but not exactly who owes whom. After a shock, those totals may leave a range of possible losses. Which extra reports would narrow that range?

This candidate shows why the answer depends on four choices: the accounting constraints, the reports you allow, the meaning of uncertainty, and the amount of reporting error.

Here is the simplest idea. Three nonnegative shares must add to one. The loss depends on the first share, but the menu allows reports only of the other two. Either report alone can leave the full worst-case loss range. Together, they determine the missing share. Allow a direct report of that first share, and the problem changes completely.

A synthetic financial network realises this example with one interbank layer and a linear loss function. So nonlinear contagion is not necessary for reports to be complementary. The accounting constraint and the restricted menu can be enough.

The paper then gives exact linear-programming certificates for the remaining loss range in a broader affine simplex model, including bounded reporting errors. A separate layered family explains how magnitude, prior beliefs and unequal reporting precision change the results. Worst-case and average uncertainty must not be confused.

These are theoretical results for declared models. They do not show how often the effect occurs in real banks, establish welfare benefits, or validate a disclosure policy. Standard information theory and linear-programming duality remain explicit antecedents.
