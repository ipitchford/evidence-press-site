Where noise can reach. An Evidence Press audio briefing. Covariance describes how different quantities fluctuate together. Wishart processes model covariances that evolve randomly over time. In an infinite-dimensional model, however, writing down an equation does not guarantee that a suitable process exists. This candidate paper asks exactly which parameters and initial covariances are possible. Its key idea is to follow where the noise travels under the deterministic evolution. Noise entering through just one direction can eventually reach infinitely many directions. When that happens, the candidate classification forces the model parameter to be an integer, with a corresponding bound on the initial covariance rank. A separate continuity condition on a Gaussian process is also necessary. When the noise remains within a finite-dimensional subspace, the paper gives a different threshold, which also counts initial covariance outside that subspace. The argument combines established finite-dimensional Wishart results with a singular-scale reduction and explicit constructions. It also gives a counterexample to one precisely identified operator-order assumption in an earlier paper. This does not invalidate all of that paper's results. The proposed classification could help researchers check whether covariance models are mathematically admissible. It does not establish improved option pricing or empirical performance.
