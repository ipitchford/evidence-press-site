An evolutionary tree describes lineages that branch. A network also allows a lineage to have two possible parents. Could that extra connection become completely invisible in the genetic patterns we observe?

This candidate studies a network with four observed species and one cycle. The result says that, under precise positivity and invertibility assumptions, its full probability distribution cannot be matched even by a mixture of two tree classes sharing the same branching shape.

The key is a mathematical quantity called matrix rank. Group the four species into two pairs and arrange their pattern probabilities in a matrix. Every split of the network has rank at least ten. But two tree classes on the same topology have rank at most eight on their shared split. Ten cannot fit into eight.

The paper proves that the network bound is sharp. It also treats smaller triangular cycles under a shared, stationary irreducible reversible mutation model, including the standard four-state GTR model. That part uses decay rates and a third moment when all nonzero rates coincide.

The evidence combines written proofs with exact algebra checks, boundary examples and internal audits. These checks do not replace external specialist review. The result concerns exact distributions, not a guarantee that a short genetic sequence will reveal the network. Mixtures of different tree shapes and more complicated histories remain separate questions.
