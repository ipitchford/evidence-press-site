An optimal transport plan describes how two distributions are paired. Adding entropy makes that pairing more diffuse. But how close is the resulting plan to the one selected when the entropy penalty disappears?

This unrefereed, AI-assisted mathematical candidate studies Gaussian distributions. Its focus is geometry: especially what happens when the distributions live on different lower-dimensional supports.

The familiar full-rank coefficient and its sharp bound were already given in public MathDB proofs. This paper credits that work. Its candidate contribution concerns singular supports, sharp angle-dependent bounds, and the difference between pointwise and uniform convergence.

Here is the key example. Imagine two lines that are almost perpendicular. Their small positive cosine measures how weakly the transport cost connects them. The error is governed by entropy divided by that cosine, not by entropy alone. A small entropy parameter can therefore leave a substantial difference between the joint plans.

The paper proves this can also happen along a family of strictly positive covariance matrices with bounded total variance. It then gives an explicit uniform error bound when the nonzero covariance eigenvalues and positive support cosines stay bounded away from zero. A simple rank-one calculation shows why the angle dependence cannot disappear.

Exactly perpendicular directions are treated separately: entropy selects independence there. The results concern Gaussian joint-plan distance, not every transport method, data-estimation error, or a practical optimal tuning rule.
