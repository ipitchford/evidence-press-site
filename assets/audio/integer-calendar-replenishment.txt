From factor two to one over log two: integer calendars for recurring deliveries. An Evidence Press briefing. First, the background. Picture a depot that restocks cash machines or fuel tanks along a branching road network. Each client holds enough stock for a fixed number of days, so it must be visited at least once in every window of that length. Each day a vehicle leaves the depot and returns. Visiting clients on the same road on the same day saves money, but an early visit resets that client's clock, so its next deadline comes sooner too. In 2022, Bosman and colleagues built calendars that cost at most twice the best possible long-run average, and asked whether the factor two could be improved. Now, the main finding. It can. Give every client an internal clock taken from one shared ladder of periods, choose the ladder's position by a logarithmic averaging argument, and round each clock's event times, not its period, up to whole days. No deadline is ever missed, clients sharing a road stay synchronised, and the average cost is at most one over the natural log of two, about one point four four, times the best possible. The calendar repeats within the longest deadline, and for calendars with that property this factor is exactly right. There is a catch: a client's gaps may alternate, for example two days then one. If every client must instead keep a constant gap, with the gaps dividing one another, the best possible factor rises to about one point six. That is still better than two. The limits: the results are mathematical, all test instances are synthetic, review so far is internal and AI-assisted, and a 2025 master's thesis with a similar title could not be obtained, so priority is not claimed.
