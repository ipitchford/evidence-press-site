Can a network and its opposite both have very weak connections?

A graph represents a network of points and links. Its complement keeps the same points but reverses which pairs are linked. A conjecture asks whether at least one of these two graphs must clear a particular spectral threshold: a mathematical test for weakly connected regions.

This candidate establishes that threshold for a broad structural class. Divide the points into two groups. If every point connects to more than half the points in the other group, the bound follows. The proof also identifies every equality case: one central point joined to two equally sized complete groups.

The work then goes beyond that simple counting rule. A weighted certificate lets different points carry different amounts of responsibility. It is checked by a linear feasibility problem, using classical matrix methods.

An explicit infinite family separates the two approaches. Start with two equal groups and connect every point across. Remove several links from one point. These examples admit neither a majority partition nor the corresponding minority partition, but the weighted certificate still succeeds. Their exact spectrum is elementary; the contribution is showing what the certificate can handle.

The paper also gives an exact test for adding a pendant point, connected by just one link. Exact integer checks cover every graph on five through nine points, alongside a separately recorded list of ten-point candidates.

The general conjecture remains open. These are sufficient conditions, explicit examples and reusable checks, not a complete solution. The work has not been independently reproduced or externally peer reviewed.
