Imagine a particle gliding across a flat table, pushed around by a force field. Its motion is called integrable when something besides the energy is also conserved. Integrable motion is regular and predictable; without that second conserved quantity, the motion can turn chaotic.

For force fields given by simple polynomials, classical mechanics knows many integrable cases. But what happens when the kinetic energy follows Einstein's relativity instead of Newton's formula?

Przybylska, Szumiński and Maciejewski showed that relativity is destructive for almost every classical integrable case. They suggested that only two kinds of potential survive: those that depend on a single coordinate, and those that are perfectly round. They settled the smallest degrees, three to five, by comparing with the classical lists, and left the general question open.

This release proves their suggestion for every polynomial degree from three upwards, so the result is new beyond those smallest degrees. A real potential of this kind is integrable exactly when, after turning the axes, it is a power of one coordinate, or a power of the distance from the centre. Only potentials with a continuous symmetry survive, and the extra conserved quantity is the matching momentum.

The proof looks along the direction in which the potential is strongest. There, straight-line motions exist, and the small wobbles across them are governed by a single number, which can only take its two simplest values at that direction. In either case, following nearby orbits once around a loop produces two independent periods, and an integrable system cannot allow that unless the potential has the symmetry.

The same reasoning settles most potentials with complex coefficients, and shows that a generic polynomial potential is never integrable in the relativistic model.

Some limits matter. The conserved quantities considered are those that extend to the complex version of the motion, so smooth quantities on a restricted region are not ruled out, and two complex families remain open.
