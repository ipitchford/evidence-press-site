Imagine a deck split into two equal groups. Cards in one group are chosen for swaps less often than cards in the other. As the deck approaches randomness, what information about its starting order remains? This candidate gives an answer for the whole permutation, not just one convenient statistic. Asymptotically, the remaining discrepancy is captured by two counts: fixed cards in the slow group and fixed cards in the fast group. With a fixed bias, only the slow count contributes to the limiting distance from randomness. As the bias disappears, both counts can matter. The transition occurs when the bias is comparable to one divided by the logarithm of the deck size. The proof connects three pieces. It identifies the joint fixed-point counts, controls the complete spectral contribution of the shuffle, and shows that a density depending on those counts accounts for the full limiting norm. Counting fixed cards alone would not prove that last step. The result applies to independent sampling of labels with replacement in two equal classes, using the stated discrete-time clock. It does not cover arbitrary weights or bias tending toward its extreme value. Finite decks can retain additional information. The package includes a written proof, exact finite checks, source comparisons and internal editorial records. Computer checks do not certify the asymptotic theorem.
