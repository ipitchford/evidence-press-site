Can three matrices cancel strongly even when each pair shows very little cancellation?

The Schatten–Hlawka problem asks whether pairwise information controls the total, using one multiplier that works for matrices of every size. This AI-assisted, unrefereed research note studies how that multiplier must deteriorate near the trace norm.

The example is small: two projectors and one negative diagonal matrix. It is a known construction, due to Ezzeri Esa, not a newly discovered family. Its three-way cancellation is proportional to a small parameter, while its pairwise cancellation is much smaller.

Moving the norm's exponent just above one adds a competing logarithmic term. The paper calculates the resulting growth rate and proves that every best parameter within this family lies near three quarters of the exponent's distance from one. It also finds the first correction and compares other fixed cancellation amplitudes.

The important limitation is the direction of the result. This is a lower bound on how large a universal multiplier must be. It is not an upper bound that safely controls arbitrary matrices. A different construction could force faster growth.
