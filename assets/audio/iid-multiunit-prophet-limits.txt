How close can online choices get to hindsight? An Evidence Press audio briefing. Imagine seeing a sequence of offers, one at a time. You know the probability distribution, but you cannot see the next offer. Once you reject an offer, it is gone. You may accept at most a fixed number of them. A prophet, by contrast, sees the whole sequence and takes the best offers. The question is how much of the prophet's expected total an optimal online strategy can guarantee, even for the hardest distribution. The offers must be independent draws from the same known distribution, with nonnegative values and a finite positive average. This research candidate proves that, for every fixed number of allowed choices, the worst-case ratio approaches a limit as the sequence becomes longer. It characterises that limit by balancing selection strategies against difficult value distributions. For two choices, certified bounds put the limit between eighty-three point six nine five percent and eighty-three point seven three zero percent. This is a narrow interval, not an exact constant, and not a promise for every finite sequence length. The proof connects a continuous-time model back to finite sequences. Explicit strategy and distribution witnesses are checked using arithmetic that bounds rounding errors. The work also shows that one earlier two-choice lower-bound construction does not reach the asymptotic optimum. It does not settle the worst ratio over all sequence lengths or provide new numerical constants for larger selection budgets. This is AI-assisted, unrefereed mathematics, not a study of real purchasing or hiring decisions. The manuscript and checking materials are the route to inspecting the evidence.
