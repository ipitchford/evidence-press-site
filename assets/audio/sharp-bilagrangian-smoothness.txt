When can a family of mathematical choices form a smooth space, without singular points? This candidate studies subspaces constrained by two alternating forms. Such pairs appear in the linear algebra behind bi-Hamiltonian systems. The collection of simultaneous Lagrangian subspaces is called a bi-Lagrangian Grassmannian. The question is whether this parameter space is smooth. The proposed answer is precise. Decompose the pencil of forms into its standard Jordan and Kronecker blocks. Every Jordan pencil block must have dimension two. Kronecker blocks can have any allowed size. Repeated eigenvalues are not a problem: in the smooth case, the answer is a product of familiar Lagrangian Grassmannians. To prove necessity, the paper examines one larger Jordan block. Near a carefully chosen subspace, explicit coordinates reveal a classical singular surface, together with some free coordinates. An involution isolates that block inside the full problem. Its fixed set transfers the obstruction to arbitrary mixtures of blocks. The paper also treats real forms, including nonreal conjugate eigenvalues. The proposed criterion and the single-block singularity were already present in earlier research. This contribution is an explicit proof of the complete smoothness criterion, not a claim to have invented those ingredients. It does not solve the entire orbit-classification programme or prove nonlinear integrability. Exact symbolic checks and deliberate corruptions support inspection of the calculation, but they are not a formal verification of the universal proof.
