When event counts have a Brownian limit. An Evidence Press briefing. Imagine a queue that moves up or down one state at a time, while a detector records only some transitions. How much do the recorded totals fluctuate over a long observation period? For an infinite-state birth and death chain, this candidate gives an exact mathematical boundary. A series built from cumulative imbalance across the edges is finite precisely when the stationary variance grows at a finite asymptotic rate. If the series diverges, variance divided by time diverges too. When it is finite, the rescaled count process approaches Brownian motion from every fixed starting distribution. But there is an important distinction. Weak convergence does not automatically imply that variances converge. The paper constructs a starting distribution with infinite count variance at every positive time, even though the Brownian functional limit still holds. The result also allows finite detected activity when the mean rate of all underlying jumps is infinite. A joint-count extension handles several detectors whose decisions at the same jump may be correlated. Their individual detection probabilities do not determine their joint uncertainty; the simultaneous marking law matters. Earlier work already contains related cumulative-square criteria and general jump-count limit theorems. The contribution here is the stated infinite-state, integrability and marking scope, not the invention of that whole method. The package contains analytic proofs, worked examples and exact finite-state checks. These checks test formulas, not the entire infinite-state theorem.
