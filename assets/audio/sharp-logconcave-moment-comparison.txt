When the extremal distribution changes. An Evidence Press audio briefing. How large or small can a measure of random variation be, if the variance is fixed? This paper studies a broad family called log-concave distributions. Their densities have no separated peaks, but they can be symmetric or strongly skewed. The question concerns moments: averages of powers of the distance from zero. Changing the power changes how much weight we give to large deviations. A conjecture by Melbourne, Roysdon, Tang and Tkocz predicted a surprising switch. For powers between one and about one point six eight, the smallest normalised moment comes from the symmetric Laplace distribution. Above the precise transition, but below two, a centred exponential distribution takes over. Beyond two, that same exponential family gives the largest moment instead. At exactly two, the comparison is an identity: every admissible distribution ties. This candidate supplies a proof of the predicted transition and identifies all equality cases. It builds on Eitan's existing reduction, which turns the full problem into an optimisation over a family of exponential distributions. A written argument handles sufficiently large powers. Rigorous interval calculations cover the remaining regions, including the delicate boundaries. The revised paper also explains near-equality. For a fixed power other than two, a nearly optimal distribution must be close to one of the exact extremisers. This is qualitative: it gives no explicit convergence rate, and its uniform form cannot cross the transition or the identity at two. The package includes the paper, proofs, code and replayable certificates. It remains an unrefereed computer-assisted candidate, not a formally verified theorem or an independently reproduced result.
