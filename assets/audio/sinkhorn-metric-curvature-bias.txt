How curved surfaces affect a local ruler for probability distributions. An Evidence Press briefing. Optimal transport compares distributions by the cost of moving mass. Entropic regularisation smooths that problem, with a parameter called epsilon. It also defines a local ruler for small changes in a distribution. As epsilon shrinks, that ruler should approach the Wasserstein metric. This unrefereed, AI-assisted candidate proves that limit for smooth, strictly positive densities on compact curved manifolds without boundary, using squared straight-line distance as the transport cost. The difficult step is controlling the exact Sinkhorn scaling and an inverse operator that becomes singular as epsilon tends to zero. Earlier work by Guo gives the curvature coefficient. This paper's contribution is rigorous control of the resulting inverse-metric formula, including nonuniform densities, rather than discovery of that coefficient. For each fixed smooth direction, the first correction is nonpositive: the local ruler reads slightly short. On a unit circle, each fixed Fourier mode has the first-order relative factor one minus epsilon over four. This is an asymptotic statement, not an exact formula at every epsilon. A positive correction cancels that first-order term for fixed smooth directions. It does not remove all error, and second-order accuracy is not uniform across arbitrarily high frequencies. The paper also gives convergence rates, exact sphere formulas and embedding-dependent examples. It does not establish sampling guarantees or convergence of unrestricted global geodesic distances.
