A four-state network breaks a conjecture about Markov spectra. An Evidence Press briefing. First, the background. Many systems in physics and biology hop at random between a few states, like a molecular motor or a chemical clock. The eigenvalues of the matrix of hopping rates say how fast such a system settles down and how strongly it can oscillate on the way. Oscillation needs a thermodynamic push around a cycle of states. Next, the conjecture. In 2025, Xu, Kolchinsky, Delvenne and Ito proposed an exact map of where these eigenvalues can lie: a classical shape called the Karpelevich region, stretched by the fastest escape rate and squashed vertically by the strongest push per step around any cycle. Now, the main finding. The conjecture is false. An explicit four-state network with whole-number rates has an eigenvalue that, after this rescaling, lands just outside the region, by about nine thousandths. One integer inequality proves it. For three or fewer states the conjecture holds, so four is the smallest failing size. The mechanism is simple. Adding weak resetting moves the eigenvalues only a little, but relaxes the squashing factor much more. The paper uses this to build counterexamples in every size from four upwards, and certifies five- and six-state examples in exact arithmetic, with the data printed so that readers can check it. Finally, the limits. Every counterexample links every pair of states. Whether the conjecture holds for a single ring of states, where it was tested, is still open. The violations are small, and the proved ellipse and polygon bounds are unaffected. This is an anonymous, unrefereed candidate, checked by AI-assisted review only, not peer review.
