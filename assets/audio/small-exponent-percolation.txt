Imagine points at every integer along a line. Each pair may be connected, with long connections becoming less likely as their length grows. Percolation asks when these random bonds can form an infinite connected cluster.

This paper studies the limit in which the distance penalty becomes very weak. Its parameter is the expected number of neighbours of a point. A branching approximation suggests that the critical value should approach one. But an undirected connection can take an exploration straight back to the point it just left, without discovering anything new.

The result identifies the first correction above one. It is the two-step return probability of the normalized connection kernel. For the pure power-law model, the correction is pi squared over twelve, multiplied by the square of the small exponent, with an error of cubic order.

The proof combines a lower bound that forbids immediate returns with established lace-expansion machinery and uniform Fourier estimates. The return mechanism itself is familiar from earlier work. The contribution here is control in this fixed, one-dimensional small-exponent regime and extensions to other kernels.

Those extensions show that local connections matter. Adding a fixed amount of nearest-neighbour weight changes the leading coefficient explicitly. Mixing tail exponents in a fixed positive interval gives another formula involving their mean.

These are asymptotic statements. The paper does not evaluate the error constant or give a certified threshold at a chosen positive exponent. It concerns independent, undirected bonds with an exact linear probability law, not every long-range network model. The written proof remains an unrefereed candidate.
