How many different steady states can a small reaction system support under the same rate constants?

This candidate gives a precise example: three chemical species, five reactions, and four distinct positive equilibria. Every equilibrium is nondegenerate, meaning that the local equilibrium equations have a nonsingular Jacobian. The count is supported by exact algebra, rather than a numerical plot alone.

The result extends to any number of species, starting with two. With two more reactions than species, the construction attains one more positive equilibrium than species. An earlier theorem supplied the matching upper bound.

The central contribution connects two mathematical settings. Phillipson and Rojas had already constructed a quadratic polynomial family with the maximum number of positive roots. This paper realizes that family using admissible mass-action reactions, integer product coefficients and positive rational rates. It does not claim a new abstract polynomial family.

One three-species example reduces to a curve crossing a horizontal line four times. In that example, two equilibria are locally attracting and two are saddles. Another example needs no inflow reaction and uses products containing at most ten molecules. Its four-equilibrium range is determined exactly as one rate changes.

Quadratic here limits the reactants to at most two molecules. Products may be larger. These are mathematical networks, not experimentally demonstrated chemistry, and the result does not promise four stable states or a global description of the dynamics.
