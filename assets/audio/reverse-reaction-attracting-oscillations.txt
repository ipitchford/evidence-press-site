One reverse arrow changes the dynamics. An Evidence Press briefing.

Chemical reaction networks can behave very differently when a single reaction is made reversible. This candidate gives an exact example with three species. In the full network, restoring one reverse reaction produces a Hopf bifurcation and, on the certified side of the boundary, a small attracting periodic orbit. Remove that reverse reaction and the remaining system has one positive equilibrium and converges to it globally for every positive choice of the retained rates.

That contrast matters because a recent paper proposed three broad conjectures saying, in different forms, that deleting an isolated intermediate reaction should preserve the relevant spectral behaviour. The explicit network disproves those general conjectures. It does not challenge the paper's restricted theorem, and it does not settle the dual-phosphorylation problem that motivated the work.

The manuscript also studies a one-parameter family. Exact symbolic calculations locate the Hopf boundary and classify the first Lyapunov coefficient except at one isolated positive value where the cubic coefficient vanishes. The paper does not claim a Bautin bifurcation there, an amplitude certificate, or reaction-count minimality.
