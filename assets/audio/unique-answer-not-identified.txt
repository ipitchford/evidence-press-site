This synthesis asks a simple question: when a model returns one answer, did the observations select it, or did a prior, constraint, penalty, calibration target, or software convention do the selecting? The paper compares four fields that use different language but face related inverse problems: age, period and cohort models; epidemic severity and case ascertainment; sign-restricted structural vector autoregressions; and aerosol forcing versus climate sensitivity. In the first three simplified observation maps, different structural states can produce exactly the same observables. The climate example is deliberately weaker: it is a practical, model-conditional ridge, not a universal exact symmetry. The central tool is a Selector Ledger. It records what the observations identify, the equivalence class or weak direction that remains, every rule that narrows it, and which conclusions survive alternative defensible rules. A bounded audit of sixteen records illustrates five recurring interpretation risks, but it is not a prevalence study and does not rank fields or researchers. The package also includes deterministic toy checks and negative controls. These verify the encoded examples, not the cited empirical models. The practical lesson is not to ban priors, regularisation, restrictions, or calibration. It is to state what they contribute, report identified sets or selector sensitivity when needed, and collect observations that load differently on the unresolved direction. This audio uses a synthetic voice and is a communication aid, not additional evidence.
