Suppose two quantum states are close. How different can their entropies be?

There is a subtle trap. Knowing the exact distance between states is not the same as knowing an upper bound on that distance. An error budget allows smaller distances too—and one of those can produce the larger entropy change.

This AI-assisted, unrefereed research candidate makes that distinction explicit. In a four-dimensional example, with the operator distance capped at one fifth, a trace-distance budget of three tenths is most demanding at an actual distance of one fifth. Substituting the budget directly into the exact-distance formula would underestimate the possible change.

The paper gives a finite calculation for the correct bound. Instead of searching over all quantum states, it compares specified endpoints and quadratic roots.

Its other main question is structural. If a pair almost achieves the largest possible entropy difference, must it resemble an exactly optimal pair? At a fixed positive trace distance, the paper proves that both states are close to one common optimal pair. The error scales with the square root of the entropy shortfall. That exponent cannot generally be improved.

There are important limits. The coefficient deteriorates as the trace distance approaches zero. The work is finite-dimensional, and its calculations are checks, not substitutes for the proofs. Published entropy inequalities supply the foundation; the additional work concerns equality, upper-bound evaluation and stability.
