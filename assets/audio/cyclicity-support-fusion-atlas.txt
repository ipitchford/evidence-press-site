Start with a polynomial phase P and the exponential integral generated by one form, dx. Earlier versions showed that its cyclic rank can be read from the inverse of P at infinity: split the inverse series into congruence channels and count the nonzero ones. Relative to named monodromy and finite-group classifications, version zero point six used that bridge to classify every fixed-seed phase of rank at most three and every polynomial extension that preserves rank. Version zero point seven brings the theory into one combined paper and changes the seed. For any polynomial amplitude A dx, choose a primitive G with derivative A. The new theorem says that the induced rank is exactly the number of non-trace channels in G composed with the inverse branch. Rank zero occurs precisely when G is a polynomial in P. The same Fourier picture gives an exact test for vanishing on any specified zero-dimensional cycle. For ordinary endpoint moments, the candidate combines the established Pakovich–Muzychuk moment theorem with the Atlas right-factor list. This yields a family-by-family classification for every phase whose fixed-seed rank is at most three. Laurent phases require a different boundary. If a local inverse φ satisfies L of φ equals τ to the minus m, then every residue moment is one explicit coefficient of A of φ times φ prime. That criterion is exact for each specified Laurent phase and one-form, but it is not a finite classification of all Laurent pairs.
