One equilibrium. Different sharp constants. An Evidence Press briefing.

When a system contains many interacting particles, a mean-field equation describes their collective distribution. If both descriptions have the same unique equilibrium, should they have the same best constant relating entropy to its dissipation? This mathematical candidate gives a precise counterexample.

The model places particles on a circle, with a smooth repulsive cosine interaction. Its uniform distribution is the unique free-energy minimizer. But two kinds of disturbance behave differently.

The ordinary particle inequality must allow a disturbance that singles out just one particle. Rotation symmetry makes that particle's marginal exactly uniform, even though the particles are not independent. This tagged test keeps the particle constant at most one, however many particles there are.

The nonlinear mean-field problem controls an entire density. A global argument shows that its optimal constant is strictly greater than one. The interaction penalises the first Fourier mode, while the other modes cannot approach the same baseline. Crucially, the proof excludes all potentially troublesome densities, not just small perturbations or a few numerical examples.

The revision gives an explicit conservative gap and sharper upper-bound examples. It does not determine the exact optimum or a particle limit. The result uses unrestricted particle disturbances and concerns a specifically identified preprint formulation. It does not settle symmetric-only tests or disprove weaker inequalities with a sublinear additive error.
