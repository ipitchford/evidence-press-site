Averages can balance while large fluctuations do not. This unrefereed, AI-assisted research candidate asks how uneven those fluctuations can become in a reversible random process.

Reversible means that, in equilibrium, a pair of successive observations has the same distribution when its order is reversed. Suppose also that the expected next observation is a negative fraction of the current one. A positive value therefore predicts a negative average next step, and vice versa.

The paper bounds the imbalance between positive and negative moments: averages of powers of their magnitudes. For every real exponent at least two, it gives an exact limit determined by that negative fraction.

A two-state example makes the bound tangible. One state has value one. The other has value minus one half. From the positive state, the process always moves to the negative state. From the negative state, it either returns or stays, with equal probabilities.

In equilibrium, the positive state occurs one third of the time. The negative state occurs two thirds. Their contributions to the ordinary signed average cancel. But their cubic moments do not. The positive cubic moment is one third; the negative cubic moment is one twelfth. That is a ratio of four, exactly attaining the new bound.

The proof also determines the universal exponent range for these reversible pairs. That classification does not automatically extend to graphs.

The graph application concerns spectral energies: sums of powers of positive and negative adjacency eigenvalues. It extends a known square-energy homomorphism bound to every real exponent above two, for graphs mapping into an edge-transitive target. A related bound uses strict vector colourings. The stronger conjecture about ordinary vector colourings remains unresolved here.
