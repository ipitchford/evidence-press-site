How much smoothing does a staged waiting time have? An Evidence Press briefing. Imagine a job that passes through several slow stages, one after another, and then one last fast stage whose speed is itself random. The total time has a probability density, and mathematicians measure how well behaved such a density is by its Pólya frequency order. Order two is the familiar property called log-concavity; each higher order is a stronger guarantee that smoothing with this density can never add extra wiggles to a signal. This paper shows that with m slow stages, each slower than every possible speed of the last stage, the order is exactly m plus one, never more, as long as the last speed is genuinely random. If the last speed is fixed, the classical answer applies and there is no limit. The same number, m plus one, turns out to be the largest size of random matrix that can carry the same law, in a sense that generalises the gamma and Wishart distributions. As an application, the paper proves a conjecture posed by Suvrit Sra in a preprint dated the twenty-ninth of October twenty twenty-six, describing exactly which parameters of a certain q-product give matrix laws. Sra had already proved some parts; the remaining part is proved here. The paper also relaxes both hypotheses separately, and shows by an exact example that some ordering of the rates is needed.
