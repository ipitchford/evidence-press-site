What two natural five-variable routes cannot do. An Evidence Press briefing. The weak Markus–Yamabe problem asks whether a polynomial vector field can look stable at every point and yet have more than one equilibrium. In five real dimensions the decisive question remains open. This paper does not claim to solve it. Instead it removes three tempting routes with exact, inspectable evidence. The first route starts from a known six-dimensional counterexample and tries to compress two auxiliary variables in parallel. The authors write down the complete polynomial family allowed by the stated bigrading. An exact rational certificate shows that no choice of its 17 free parameters gives the required characteristic polynomial. The second route places the two auxiliaries in a chain with unequal weights. Again the displayed inventory is complete within that architecture. Here the final contradiction becomes unusually transparent: eight affine-linear coefficient equations force all remaining variables, and a ninth equation then has residual 16,367,616 instead of zero. A separate standard-library checker replays that short argument. The third route comes from the Hessian side of the Jacobian problem. For a displayed family of potentials, with three auxiliary variables and nonzero parameters kappa, r and t, the paper extracts the nonconstant coefficient matrices of the Hessian. Fifteen selected matrices have determinant minus 1,304,596,316,160 times kappa to the fifteenth, r to the tenth and t to the sixth. They therefore span the entire space of symmetric 5 by 5 matrices. This rules out making the Jacobian trace constant through invertible affine changes of source and target within that whole auxiliary-affine family. The evidence is exact rather than numerical. Ordinary and optimized Python replay both passed. Four deliberately corrupted certificates were rejected. Separate audits checked the monomial inventories, the short chain contradiction and the symbolic Hessian determinant. These checks establish the encoded algebra and the stated family coverage. They do not prove that every conceivable five-dimensional construction fails. They are not proof-assistant formalisation, external specialist review or independent external reproduction. The most important conclusion is therefore two-sided. Several natural compression and Hessian-normalisation strategies are now closed with reusable certificates, but the global five-dimensional weak Markus–Yamabe problem remains open.
